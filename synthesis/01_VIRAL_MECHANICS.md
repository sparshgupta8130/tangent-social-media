# Viral Mechanics — Final Synthesis (post-Gate-C)

Evidence base: 18 Gate-B-passed creator dossiers (8 YouTube, 10 Instagram-wave) built on ~190
per-video nine-section post-mortems, a 24-account logged-in Instagram harvest, a research-graded
psychology/algorithm rubric (03_virality_science.md, citations independently verified), two
adversarial critic audits, and a 110-reel remediation re-pull. Every claim below cites its source
dossier(s). Confidence: HIGH unless noted.

## Part 0 — The measurement layer (read this before believing any "viral" number)

**Law 0.1 — Leaderboards lie three ways.** (a) Paid boosts: sponsor-funded (Wes Roth HeyGen
0.06% LR; Futurepedia Grammarly 3.2M @ 0.082%), self-funded undisclosed (kallaway 35-74M spikes @
0.0107-0.088% corrected LR — 15-400x below his own organic baseline), partial (tiffintech Sanofi
0.061%), and paid reach-amplification with normal
engagement (tiffintech Lenovo 73.7M @ 1.55% — real engagement, bought distribution; 5,900x
same-script IG-vs-TikTok gap). (b) Pins: date-verified legacy hits pinned above current content
(nateherkai, thevarunmayya ×3, handlytech, kallaway, tech.unicorn, godofprompt). (c) Instrument
error: our own browser-harvest like counts were wrong on 15.5% of 110 re-pulled reels.
**Operational rule: no exemplar is "organic viral" until it passes a like-rate sanity check
(~1-10% band) from a second independent tool, plus a date check against pin position.**

**Law 0.2 — The views×like-rate decomposition diagnoses WHY a video failed.** High LR + low views
= hook/discovery failure (Berman's OpenClaw flop: 4.56% LR vs the hit's 2.50%, 85x fewer views;
Riley Brown's flops; sebastien's flops out-engaging 3 of 5 tops). Low LR + low views = craft
failure (Berman's "ways to use ai": dead 14s preamble). Treat every flop autopsy as one or the
other; the fixes are different.

## Part 1 — The winning mechanics

**1. The one-second, result-first hook.** Every top performer across all 8 YT dossiers names or
shows the payoff in second 0-1; zero preamble, zero branding. Shorts feeds are swipe-gated
(YouTube's own "viewed vs swiped away" metric; Jenny Hoyos' one-second rule — rubric §hooks).
Flop signature: abstract or delayed openings (Skill Leap's 2 flops; Futurepedia's Dropbox flop
buried the tool name 10s deep; Berman's worst had a 14s preamble). Hook classes that won, ranked
by frequency in tops: demo-in-motion/result-first > bold specific claim > news-event framing >
question (questions only when resolved — see Law 4).

**2. Topic selection dominates format execution.** The corpus's cleanest natural experiments:
Nate Herk's rank-161 flop uses the IDENTICAL template as his 1.34M #1, posted 3 days apart —
"B2B sales-email approval" vs "autonomous AI team" decided a 180x gap (yt_nateherk §C). Liam
Ottley's flops match his hits on hook taxonomy but address niche verticals instead of "anyone who
wants AI money." Sisinty's tops are all news topics; his flops are niche tool demos with
equal engagement rates. **Selector formula: universally-relatable premise (or a universal frame
wrapped around a niche topic — Skill Leap's iPhone-frame nearly 3x'd their pure-AI median) +
concrete verifiable payoff.** Skill Leap's dossier names the underlying law the **AI-topic reach
ceiling**: pure-AI framing caps reach even when per-viewer resonance is HIGHER (their AI shorts
out-liked their 1M-view hit, 4.01% vs 3.32%) — the FRAME, not the topic, sets the ceiling.

**3. Payoff intensity, not checklist presence.** STEPPS pillars being "present" predicts nothing;
their INTENSITY does (yt_nateherk flop: every pillar present at low intensity). Target emotion is
high-arousal awe (Berger & Milkman, verified citation): AI-capability awe is the highest organic
ceiling on Wes Roth's and Futurepedia's channels (their biggest organic hits are an AI skit and a
capability demo — not tutorials, on tutorial channels). Payoffs that worked: exact dollar figures,
an email actually sent and read aloud, a 3D render appearing, an on-camera "wow."

**4. Close every loop; escalate multi-payoffs.** Winners visibly resolve what the hook promises
(Riley Brown's #1 closes every sub-command on screen). The worst flops open questions they never
answer (Wes Roth's worst; Skill Leap's trail-off endings). For multi-item videos, escalating
listicles (most impressive LAST) beat equal-weight lists (Berman's #4, 3-5s per item).
One deliberate absurd/comedic beat inside a utilitarian demo creates the quotable share moment
(Riley's "dance around the room at 8pm").

**5. Demo-in-motion grammar for "watch AI work" content** (the genre closest to Tangent):
drop the viewer into an ALREADY-RUNNING system; granular visible state-changes every 5-15s
(not one big reveal); an honest error-and-fix or "now we wait" beat outperforms polished cuts
(yt_rileybrown); fixed-length template discipline (Devin Huynh: all five tops are the same
~24.13s structure, real product UI, no narration, high cut density — ig_elevenpercentprod).

**6. The comment-keyword DM funnel is the strongest distribution+capture mechanic in the corpus.**
Devin: comments EXCEED likes on 3 of top 5 (61,429 vs 53,167 on #1). Nate Herk IG: 7 of 9 use it,
comment:like 0.95-3.86:1 vs 0.09-0.28:1 without — a clean same-account experiment. Riley Brown's
single biggest outlier gated the "how" behind a keyword (4x his normal comment rate). Mechanism:
comments and DM-sends are exactly the signals Instagram ranks on (Mosseri, verified). The
compliant, durable variant gates a REAL deliverable delivered as promised — this is not "comment
YES" bait (which platforms penalize). Works on IG; essentially absent from the same creators' YT
grammar (single CTA to long-form there).

**7. Series packaging is a cold-start weapon, not a compounding engine.** Harper Carroll's
numbered "10 Days of AI Basics" powered 2K→200K in 3 weeks (press-verified) — but episode views
FRONT-LOAD and decline; the series' power is commitment-signaling, identity ("Day N" = appointment
viewing), and follow-conversion, not view compounding (ig_harpercarrollai §C, honest complication).
Counterexample: ai_ankitarora's organic 5.5M news hit decayed with no series wrapper to catch the
audience (trio §C). Rule: hits without a repeatable wrapper evaporate; wrappers without hits
still convert the hits you eventually get.

**8. News velocity is a reliable, engineerable lever.** Wes Roth: three top-8 shorts hard-timed
0-4 days to named events (independently date-verified). Skill Leap's AI ceiling = GPT-4 launch
day. Sisinty's entire top-5 = news hooks. Variant: recurring pain-point arbitrage (Nick Saraev's
"free Claude Code" hits — rides a recurring complaint rather than a calendar event).

**9. Sponsored content survives ONLY inside the creator's own playbook.** Harper's #ClaudePartner
posts sit in her top 5 (sponsor fit + her structure); her #ServiceNowPartner flop and Varun
Mayya's two #ad flops (10-15x organic penalty, same-account) show the mechanism: NOT algorithmic
tag-demotion (Mosseri on record denying it; flop LRs stay in-band) but sends-collapse (people
don't forward ads) plus visible trust-friction in comments (ig_thevarunmayya §C — cleanest
unconfounded read).

**10. Platform-native beats cross-posting; the lane must fit the genre.** Same-clip deltas:
Devin's #1 did >1,000x better on IG than YT; sebastien's IG beat his 558K-follower TikTok by a
182x VIEW gap on the one matched pair with view data (a corrected ~61x LIKE-RATE gap on a
different pair — two metrics, not a range); Herk's same-day cross-post flopped on both platforms
(content failure travels too). Genre-lane inversion: tool-demo content wins in shorts/reels;
art-spectacle wins LONG-FORM (Curious Refuge: top long-form 228x their top Short; even their
MEDIAN long-form beat their best Short). Choose lane per content genre, not platform fashion.

**11. Volume raises the floor, not the ceiling.** Berman (200-short sweep): bottom 75% live in a
tight 3.6-12K band; top 5% capture 52% of views; the #1 outlier is off-niche and inexplicable by
craft. Cadence is floor-insurance and trend-coverage; outliers come from selection + timing +
lottery exposure. Plan economics on the MEDIAN, treat spikes as windfalls.

## Part 2 — Failure modes (each observed ≥2 independent times)

- **Hook-coherence break**: hook promises a different video than delivered (Riley's Taylor Swift
  flop; Skill Leap's precondition-laden hooks).
- **Format abandonment**: dropping the channel's signature grammar for a sponsor read or slideshow
  (Riley's sponsor flop; handlytech's stock-photo Pippit flop — most extreme persona break).
- **Audience-mismatch death** (the handlytech autopsy): off-topic mega-virality imports followers
  who don't want your real content; distribution collapses 93-1,350x while per-viewer engagement
  stays healthy; legacy pins keep mis-setting expectations. Warning signs in order: median/follower
  ratio sliding under ~5%, hits only from off-pillar content, flat non-pinned spread.
- **Spike-decay without wrapper** (ankitarora) vs series-caught spikes (Harper).
- **Sponsored-brief capture** (Varun, Harper's ServiceNow): executing the sponsor's structure
  instead of your own.
- **Preamble/credentialing cold-opens** (Berman's worst; Devin's one explainer flop died with
  better production polish than his wins).
- **Unresolved loops** (Wes Roth's worst flop asks at 0:00, never answers).
