## A. Convergent patterns (≥3 independent creators each)

1. **Raw view counts lie; engagement signatures don't.** [FINAL, post-Gate-C-remediation]
   Verified boost cases: Wes Roth (HeyGen short, 0.06% LR), Futurepedia (Grammarly 3.2M, 0.082%),
   kallaway (self-funded, 0.0107-0.088% (corrected), 15-400x below own baseline), NetworkChuck Vanta post #1
   (0.070%, ~54-66x below norm; post #2 milder at 0.53%), tiffintech Sanofi PARTIAL boost (0.061%).
   RETRACTED: tiffintech Lenovo/FIFA as "near-zero engagement" — corrected LRs 1.55%/0.57% are in
   the normal band; those posts are paid REACH AMPLIFICATION (5,900x same-script IG-vs-TikTok gap)
   with real engagement, a distinct category from dead-engagement boosts. RULE stands: every viral
   exemplar passes a like-rate sanity check (~1-10% organic norm) via a SECOND independent tool
   (browser-scrape likes were wrong on 15.5% of 110 re-pulled reels).

2. **Flops are usually discovery/hook failures, not quality failures.** Flop like-rates rival or
   beat hits across Berman (OpenClaw pair, 85x view gap, flop LR higher), Riley Brown (both flops),
   Futurepedia (flops 1.7-3.9% LR). Diagnostic value: like-rate-vs-views decomposition separates
   "bad hook/packaging" from "bad content" per video.

3. **Topic/payoff selection dominates format execution.** Nate Herk's identical-template flop
   (0.29x median, 3 days after the 52.7x hit); Liam Ottley's craft-equal flops (breadth of
   audience-addressability decided); Skill Leap (frame universality, not topic, set reach).
   The winning selector: universally-relatable premise (or universal frame around a niche topic)
   + concrete, VERIFIABLE payoff (dollar figure, sent email, visible artifact).

4. **Result-first / demo-in-motion hooks with zero preamble** are the dominant winning hook class
   across Nate Herk, Riley Brown, Berman, Futurepedia tops; verbatim evidence in each dossier.
   Related: every top performer names the payoff in second 0-1 (Shorts one-second gate — rubric
   §hooks; YouTube's own "viewed vs swiped away" metric).

5. **Awe is the target emotion; calm-useful caps lower organically.** Wes Roth's organic ceiling =
   AI-capability awe demos; Herk's hits engineer on-camera "wow"; Curious Refuge's 3-4.4M pieces
   are pure spectacle. STEPPS refinement from Herk flop analysis: pillar PRESENCE isn't enough —
   payoff INTENSITY is the variable that matters.

6. **Close every loop you open.** Unresolved-question flops (Wes Roth's worst; Skill Leap flops
   trail off; Riley Brown closes every sub-command visibly and wins). Loop-closing is checkable
   per-video and consistently separates tops from flops.

7. **Comment-keyword DM funnels ("comment X and I'll DM you") recur across the winners**: Riley
   Brown's biggest outlier (4x comment rate), elevenpercentprod's entire top-5 ("comment Claude"),
   nateherkai IG tops. Mechanism per rubric: comments+DM-sends are top ranking fuel (Mosseri
   sends-signal); also gates the "how" to convert reach into owned audience. Tension flagged in
   rubric: platform engagement-bait policies — the winners gate a REAL deliverable, which appears
   to be the compliant/durable variant.

8. **Shorts/reels are top-of-funnel, not the business.** Every monetizing creator converts:
   Skool communities (Herk $99/mo, Sebastien $57-79/mo, Ottley AAA), SaaS (Sabrina/Blotato,
   Devin/AutoEdit, Riley/Vibecode), courses (Sisinty/GrowthSchool, Skill Leap), sponsorships
   (TiffinTech, NetworkChuck, HandlyTech). Long-form or community is where value concentrates;
   several channels (Ottley: ONE short ever; Curious Refuge/Futurepedia: dormant shorts lanes)
   barely bother with shorts.

9. **Volume raises the floor, not the ceiling** (Berman: bottom-75% in tight 3.6-12K band; top 5%
   = 52% of views). Cadence sustains; outliers are engineered lotteries (trend-timing, spectacle).

10. **Rebrand/pivot-on-signal is the origin story of multiple winners**: Skill Leap (ex-Howfinity),
    Curious Refuge (ex-general-interest tutorials — Squarespace/Notion/Canva and more, 2016-2023;
    business created by 2 accidental hits 11 days apart), Devin Huynh (videography → AI plugin
    2026). Winners noticed an outlier and rebuilt the channel around it fast. [Gate C correction:
    Futurepedia's Shorts dormancy COINCIDES with its HubSpot acquisition — correlation only, do
    not state as causal. Also: the Curious Refuge 561K "collab short" is hosted on Adobe's channel,
    not CR's — attribute accordingly if cited.]

11. **News/trend velocity is a reliable lever** (Wes Roth 0-4 day turnarounds on named events;
    Skill Leap's GPT-4 launch-day = their AI ceiling; Berman's daily news machine). Requires ops
    speed, not luck.
