# Tangent Voice — Verbal & Visual Identity

Load-bearing for G1/G2 lens 5 (CRAFT & AESTHETICS) and every asset with on-screen text, UI
chrome, or spoken/written voice. Extends the tangent.gallery identity — classy, minimal,
gallery-white, Fraunces serif display + Inter — into short-form video, where the pull toward
generic creator-economy visual language is strongest.

## Frame aesthetic — what our footage looks like

- **Real UI, no fake dashboards.** Every screen-captured interface is the genuine
  Lightroom/Claude/tool UI, unmodified for drama (`production/adobe-playbook.md`). "Prove, don't
  assert" applies to the visuals themselves [CORPUS: synthesis/02 §6].
- **Gallery-grade color.** Grading, contrast, and export settings match the standard
  tangent.gallery holds for finished work — a Reel is not exempt from the brand's quality floor
  just because it's disposable-feeling media.
- **Generous negative space in text placement.** On-screen text sits in clean, deliberate space
  (echoing Fraunces/Inter's editorial feel), never crammed edge-to-edge or stacked in competing
  boxes.
- **What we never use:** meme fonts, red circle/arrow call-out graphics, shocked-face reaction
  thumbnails, countdown-timer urgency graphics, neon drop-shadow text. These are the visual
  register of exactly the "AI slop"/hype-creator aesthetic the positioning is built against
  (`audience/positioning.md`).

## Typography & color specifics

Display/hook text on-screen: **Fraunces** (serif, the gallery's display face) — used sparingly,
for the single load-bearing line per beat (a hook statement, a critic verdict), never for dense
captions. Supporting text (captions, UI labels, disclosure lines): **Inter** — legible at small
sizes, sound-off-test-friendly (`craft/scripting-voice.md`). Background default: gallery-white
or the graded photograph itself — never a colored gradient template behind text. Accent color
comes from the photograph in frame, not a fixed brand-color chip; the image is always the
richest color in the frame, text stays neutral (black/white/gallery-white) so it never competes
with the work.

## Sound identity

No trending meme audio — it plugs into a discovery mechanic built for a different genre of
content and reads against the calm-craft tone (`craft/scripting-voice.md`). Preferred: real UI
sound (slider drags, shutter clicks, keyboard) as the primary audio texture, per the
demo-in-motion grammar (`craft/structure-and-retention.md`); an original or quiet, unobtrusive
score under B-roll only, never a driving hype-house track. If a track is licensed, credit it in
`publish.md`'s audio-credit field — silence on attribution is a hygiene-lens issue, not a taste
one.

## Thumbnail identity (YouTube)

Per the Shorts-vs-long-form packaging split (`platform/youtube.md`): Shorts thumbnails are
largely moot (the in-video hook does the work, `craft/hooks.md`), so do not spend production
effort there. Long-form thumbnails follow the same never-use list above — a still from the
gallery-grade footage itself, Fraunces title text if any, no shocked face, no red arrow.

## Voice attributes

Calm. Precise. Quietly confident. A dry wit — never a joke that undercuts the craft, but the
critic's blunt one-liners are allowed to be funny because they're true, not because they're
performing for laughs (`craft/scripting-voice.md` — quotable-beat engineering). The voice never
raises its volume to compensate for a weak payoff; if the payoff needs volume, fix the payoff.

## Relationship between surfaces

`STRATEGY.md` §1, restated for voice consistency: **IG shows** (the demo, the reach engine, in
motion) → **YT deepens** (long-form spectacle, the demographic hedge for 35+) → **gallery
proves** (the finished, static, standing evidence — the brand's quality anchor) → **email
owns** (the only asset we actually hold). Voice stays consistent across all four —
calm/precise/dry-wit — but register shifts: IG is the most kinetic, gallery the most still, YT
sits in between, email is the most direct/personal (a note from the engineer, not a
newsletter-voice broadcast).

## Naming conventions — Claude, the agents, the pipeline, on-screen

Consistent, named references compound two RESEARCH-BACKED mechanisms simultaneously: parasocial
bonding (recurring, recognizable identity across videos measurably increases trust) and mere
exposure (repeated exposure to the same face/format/phrase increases liking even without
conscious recognition) [RESEARCH-BACKED: research/03 §2.4.1, §2.4.2]. Practical rule: refer to
Claude as **Claude** on-screen, consistently — never a cutesy pet-name that obscures what tool
is actually doing the work (disclosure doctrine, `audience/positioning.md`). The critic may be
named/personified consistently as a recurring on-screen character (e.g., a lower-third label
"THE CRITIC" for its verdicts) — a stable, repeated device across episodes, not a shifting one,
so exposure compounds instead of resetting each post. Any named recurring device (the critic
label, an episode-numbering scheme, a signature transition) must be reused identically across
posts once adopted — inventing a new one per video forfeits the compounding effect entirely.
