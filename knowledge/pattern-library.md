### 2.6 Riley Brown's build format (vibe-coding demo template)

Reconstructed from Videos 1–5 ("the 3x-16x-median outlier tier" — **a range across five different videos, not one fixed ratio**).

**Core recurring shape (10 beats):**

2. Framing/context beat: personal-relatability self-disclosure, or capability statement naming tools/integrations.
3. First command/task executed live: a concrete, named real-world action. Each command is its own mini open-loop.
4. Escalation in visible stages roughly every 10–20s (new pose/skin, new mechanic, iterative refinement).
5. Honest "now we wait"/processing beat — explicit, unhidden wait time, paradoxically building credibility rather than being cut for pacing.
6. Comedic/absurd sub-task inserted among utilitarian ones (an amusement/rewatch beat).
7. Error-and-recovery beat (when present) — treated as a *stronger* trust-building device than a flawless run.
8. Payoff montage — visibly closes every loop opened.
9. Cross-device/public-deployment proof (when present) — deploys, opens on a second device — a strong rewatch-worthy beat.
10. Close/CTA: comment-gated engagement bait ("comment [MCP]"), verbal redirect to owned long-form content, next-video teaser, or an App Store link.

#### 3.14a THE SKILL LEAP REACH-CEILING FINDING (full depth, verbatim where possible)

Per `critic/FINAL_SYNTHESIS_AUDIT.md` Finding 10, this finding was entirely **omitted from all downstream synthesis documents** despite the source dossier itself flagging it as *"the dossier's most important finding for Tangent's own strategy."* It is reproduced here in full so it cannot be dropped again.

## 7. METHOD NOTES — data reliability caveats

Everything a downstream user of this corpus needs to distrust appropriately.

### 7.1 The corpus-wide browser-scrape like-count defect (the single most important method note)

**The mechanism, per `critic/GATE_C_instagram_half.md` and `critic/GATE_C_REMEDIATION.md`:** Instagram's rendered "Liked by `[named user]`, `[named user]` and N others" UI pattern causes a naive browser-DOM scraper to sometimes capture only the trailing "N others" figure rather than the true total. This explains both the unpredictable per-post magnitude (1.22x to 453.25x — depends on how many named likers happened to render that session) and why **comments were never affected in 110/110 checked reels** (Instagram renders comments as a plain "View all N comments" numeral with no named-user-prefix pattern to misparse). Dates and view-count grid scrapes were also never affected.

**Scale of the defect (full-corpus census, 110 cited reels, `GATE_C_REMEDIATION.md`):** MATCH (<1.15x) 93/110 (84.5%); DRIFT (1.15x-2.0x) 7/110 (6.4%); WRONG (>2.0x) 10/110 (9.1%). **Combined affected rate: ~15.5% corpus-wide, ~9.1% severely (>2x off)** — meaningfully lower than an earlier critic sample's ~45% headline, because that sample deliberately concentrated on already-flagged anomalies (a biased, appropriately-scoped scoping audit, not the true base rate). The error distribution is bimodal — a mild 1.2x-2x accrual-or-noise band, or a severe 7x-453x band — not a smooth curve, supporting the mechanistic (not random-noise) explanation.

**Correlation with sponsorship, but not exclusive to it:** 11 of 17 non-MATCH reels (65%) are disclosed-sponsored/suspected-boosted posts; **6 of 17 (35%) are ordinary organic content with no sponsorship signal at all** (sebastien's own top-3 videos, harpercarrollai's "Comment BUILD" tool demo, nick_saraev's organic GPT-5.5 explainer, 100xengineers' pinned legacy tile). "The bug is a scraper defect that can strike any post above a certain reach threshold, not a symptom specific to paid content — sponsorship correlates with getting caught (because sponsored posts were already being scrutinized for suspiciously low engagement) more than it causes the underlying defect."

### 7.2 Non-bug artifacts that must not be folded into the error-rate statistics

- **Instagram's "hide like count" creator setting** (distinct from the scraper bug): two harpercarrollai reels return exactly "3 likes" on repeated, independent pulls hours apart — comments remain intact and plausible (58, 536). A genuine scrape bug resolves to a much larger real number on independent re-pull, as every WRONG/DRIFT reel above did; a hidden-like-count post stays pinned at the same small placeholder. `ig_harpercarrollai.md`, `GATE_C_REMEDIATION.md` §2.
- **Genuine pinning:** confirmed pinned reels at nateherkai, thevarunmayya, handlytech, kallaway, and 100xengineers all skew raw top-N/median statistics badly and are not comparable to non-pinned organic content — always compute both a raw and a "true organic ceiling (non-pinned)" figure. Pinning is not reliably guessable from view-count shape alone; always date-check via a detail pull before trusting a "not pinned" call.
- **Boost signatures without an official disclosure tag:** kallaway's three mid-grid spikes show no official Instagram "Paid Partnership" label (two carry self-declared, non-official hashtags), leading to a self-funded-ad-spend theory rather than an undisclosed sponsor deal — a distinct pattern from tiffintech/networkchuck's officially-disclosed sponsor boosts.

### 7.3 Cross-verification methods used across the corpus, ranked by rigor demonstrated

1. **Best-practice model: Kallaway's dual-reading methodology** (trio dossier) — when the dossier's own yt-dlp pull and the harvest file's re-verification pull disagreed, the dossier reported BOTH numbers side by side, computed the conclusion under both readings, and only asserted what held true either way. Subsequent audit confirmed the dossier's own number was correct all along — the discipline of never overclaiming certainty on a disputed figure is the reusable lesson, independent of which number turned out right.
2. **Handlytech's Video 4** — deliberately built its "why it won" analysis to hold under either a 59 or 114 likes reading, a similar dual-reading design.
3. **Triple/quadruple-independent verification (Liam Ottley's "exactly one Short" finding):** yt-dlp with two different tool versions, raw `ytInitialData` JSON inspection, and a live browser click-through, plus an arithmetic cross-check (250 long-form + 1 short = 251, matching the channel header) — the single most rigorous piece of verification methodology in the corpus.
4. **Third-method triangulation (Vaibhav Sisinty):** WebFetch (IG) + yt-dlp (YouTube proxy) + the original browser-harvest, landing within 1-4% of each other.
5. **Cautionary counter-example (Sebastien Jefferies):** cross-verifying a same-day cross-post's numbers on a *different platform* (TikTok/YouTube) is not a substitute for directly re-pulling the number under scrutiny from its own primary source (IG). This is the corpus's one dossier that skipped the IG-native re-check that 6+ peer dossiers used, and it is the direct, predictable cause of its three wrong like-rate claims.

### 7.4 Evidence-tier discipline — good and bad examples found in the corpus

- **Exemplary [INFERRED]-vs-[OBSERVED] discipline:** Matthew Berman's Video 1 has no available transcript at all (`yt-dlp --list-subs` confirms zero captions exist) — the dossier downgrades every claim about it to [INFERRED] rather than inventing a hook explanation, stating directly: *"I am flagging that honestly rather than inventing a hook-based explanation I cannot support."* Wes Roth's #1 short is tagged [INFERRED paid-boost, not organic] every single time it recurs across the document, never upgraded to fact on repetition.
- **Honest uncertainty as a documented pattern (Futurepedia Video 2):** 3.2M views but a 0.082% like-rate (30-50x lower than every other video) — the dossier names 2-3 plausible mechanisms (paid amplification, autoplay/embed consumption) without picking a favored one: *"No source confirms which mechanism is at play — this is flagged as an open question, not resolved."*
- **Attribution-before-citing lesson (Curious Refuge's Adobe Firefly collab):** a "561K views" figure sourced from a creator's own About-page Collaborations shelf is *accurate*, but the video is hosted on the *partner's* YouTube channel, not the creator's own — a number appearing in a creator's self-description doesn't mean it belongs to that creator's own channel-performance statistics. Always check the actual `channel` field before citing a collaboration's view count as the host creator's own data point.
- **Causal-vs-correlational hedge discipline (Futurepedia's Shorts dormancy):** the source dossier ties the acquisition and the Shorts stoppage together only as a *timing correlation*, explicitly not a confirmed causal claim — a downstream synthesis document overstated this to a confirmed fact; the corpus's own primary-source hedge must always be preserved.

### 7.5 Standing per-dossier scope limitations (not data errors, but caveats on generalizability)

- **Vaibhav Sisinty's dossier is explicitly NOT a full deep-dive** — grid-only, reused from an earlier prototype pass with 7 supplementary detail-pulls added post-hoc.
- **Riley Brown's 39-video Shorts sample is a confirmed YouTube-shelf ceiling, not his full lifetime Shorts history** — both a 1:150 and a 1:300 flat-playlist pull returned the identical 39 videos; every median-relative statistic in that dossier should be read as "median of this sample," not a verified population median.
- **Nate Herk's (YouTube) cadence claims rest on only 10 of 14 planned date-samples** — 4 were never recovered after rate-limiting and are logged as a genuine unfilled gap, not silently interpolated.
- **Devin Huynh's 156% efficiency ratio is n=1** in the 22-account corpus for accounts under ~300K followers — see `critic/FINAL_SYNTHESIS_AUDIT.md` Finding 6; never cite it as a corroborated small-account pattern.
- **The Skill Leap AI-topic reach-ceiling finding (§3.14a) was entirely omitted from every downstream synthesis document** despite being flagged by its own source dossier as the most important finding for the client's strategy — this is the corpus's clearest documented case of a load-bearing finding getting lost between dossier and synthesis; do not let it happen again in this pattern library or anything built from it.
