## 1. Mental availability & category entry points

**Claim:** buyers — and viewers — choose from memory, not from in-the-moment comparison
shopping. Brands (and accounts) grow by being the thing memory serves up at more of the
situations that trigger category thinking ("category entry points," CEPs), not by deepening
loyalty among people who already think of them. [CANON: Sharp, *How Brands Grow*, Oxford
University Press, 2010; Romaniuk & Sharp, *How Brands Grow Part 2*, 2015; Ehrenberg-Bass
Institute for Marketing Science] [VERIFIED-ONLINE:
https://marketingscience.info/news-and-insights/category-entry-points-the-last-two-strategic-pitfalls-unraveled,
2026-07-31].

**What it means for Tangent:** our entry points are not "I want to buy fine-art
photography" — they're the viewing/need moments a scrolling stranger is already in: *"how
do I edit this photo," "AI did something insane," "I want my photos to look
professional," "is this AI slop or actually good."* Growth = becoming the account memory
serves at more of these moments, not deepening the follower base that already exists. The
full entry-point map, with tier assignments, lives in `positioning-doctrine.md` — this
section supplies the underlying research, that file does the operational mapping.

## 2. Distinctive assets vs. differentiation [CONTESTED]

**Claim (Sharp/Romaniuk school):** distinctive assets — unique, ownable, recognizable
identifiers (a color, a sound, a face, a phrase) — compound recognition over time and are
the more reliably provable growth lever; claimed *differentiation* ("we're better/different
because X") is weakly supported as a growth driver because most buyers don't perceive or
weight the differences marketers assert. [CANON: Sharp, *How Brands Grow*, 2010; Romaniuk,
Sharp & Ehrenberg, "Evidence concerning the Importance of Perceived Brand Differentiation,"
*Australasian Marketing Journal*, 2007] [VERIFIED-ONLINE:
https://journals.sagepub.com/doi/abs/10.1016/S1441-3582(07)70042-3, 2026-07-31].

**Counter-claim (positioning school):** Ries & Trout's positioning canon holds
differentiation is the entire point — a brand survives by owning a distinct, defensible
position in the mind, not merely by being recognizable. [CANON: Ries & Trout, *Positioning:
The Battle for Your Mind*, 1981].

**[CONTESTED]:** these schools genuinely disagree, unresolved in the field — this file does
not adjudicate it. [VERIFIED-ONLINE: https://www.marketingweek.com/ritson-differentiation-uniqueness/,
2026-07-31] — Ritson's synthesis ("distinctiveness gets you noticed, differentiation gets
you chosen") is a practitioner reconciliation attempt, not a resolved peer-reviewed finding.

**The Brand Office's working synthesis:** distinctive-asset discipline
(`distinctive-assets.md`) is non-negotiable from day one — it costs nothing to be
consistent and the compounding evidence is comparatively strong. Differentiation *claims*
are earned only where provable, never asserted rhetorically — and for Tangent this isn't a
hedge: the agentic-editing pipeline (Claude driving Lightroom, critic-gated, on Sparsh's own
raws) is a differentiation claim we can show on screen every time, exactly the case the
positioning school's argument was built for. "Prove, don't assert" [REPO: STRATEGY.md §2]
is how Tangent gets to claim differentiation without leaning on the mechanism Sharp
critiques (asserted-but-unperceived difference) — we're not claiming it, we're filming it.

## 3. Reach over loyalty — the double jeopardy law

**Claim:** small/low-share brands are punished twice — fewer buyers AND those buyers are
slightly less frequent/loyal than a big brand's buyers. A near-universal empirical
regularity across hundreds of categories, confirmed in B2B contexts too. Growth for a small
brand comes overwhelmingly from **penetration** — reaching new, often light or one-time
viewers — not from squeezing more out of the existing base; loyalty rises mostly as a
side-effect of growing share, not the reverse. [CANON: Ehrenberg, Goodhardt & Barwise,
"Double Jeopardy: A Dirichlet Theory," 1990; Ehrenberg-Bass Institute for Marketing
Science] [VERIFIED-ONLINE:
https://marketingscience.info/the-double-jeopardy-law-in-b2b-shows-the-way-to-grow/,
2026-07-31; https://marketingscience.info/news-and-insights/what-causes-the-double-jeopardy-law,
2026-07-31].

**What it means for Tangent:** at 0 followers, effort belongs in reach — reels built to
stop a cold stranger, HERO-tier spectacle — not in over-serving the small existing base with
insider lore or community-only material. The funnel [REPO: STRATEGY.md §5] exists to
*capture* reach into something owned; it does not replace the reach-generation job.
Operational rule for the Brand Office at G3: a brief optimized for "give the existing followers
more of what they already love" over "would this stop a cold stranger" is a flag — early
growth math runs on new light viewers, not repeat engagement from the base.

## 4. The 95-5 rule [EXTRAPOLATION for creator context]

**Claim:** in the B2B context studied, roughly 95% of the addressable buyer population is
out-of-market at any given time (illustrative case: corporate service-provider switching
averages once every ~5 years, implying ~5% "in market" per quarter). The marketing job for
that 95% is not persuading an immediate purchase — it's building the memory structures
(mental availability) recalled once they eventually enter the market. [CANON: Dawes, John —
Ehrenberg-Bass Institute, published via LinkedIn's B2B Institute, 2021] [VERIFIED-ONLINE:
https://marketingscience.info/news-and-insights/the-955-rule-is-the-new-6040-rule,
2026-07-31 — this source states plainly that Dawes's own percentages "aren't meant to be
interpreted literally"; the number is illustrative, not a measured constant].

**Provenance, explicit:** this is B2B research about purchase-cycle timing (banks, law
firms, enterprise software). **[EXTRAPOLATION]** applying it to a solo creator's audience:
there is no equivalent "in-market" state for following a photography/AI account the way
there is for switching a bank — most of a cold-scroll audience isn't "in-market" to follow
anyone, in any category, ever, at any single moment, which is a different (and less
bounded) phenomenon than B2B purchase-cycle timing. What transfers directionally, and is
the real operational takeaway: most people a Tangent reel reaches will never follow on that
view — one-off value plus memorable distinctive assets that get recognized on a *later*
scroll matter more than a hard CTA on any single post. **Do not quote "95%" or "5%" as a
Tangent-specific number** in any brief or memo — it is a B2B illustrative figure, not a
measured Tangent or creator-economy statistic.

## 5. Brand equity models — diagnostic vocabulary, not gospel

**Keller's CBBE pyramid:** four sequential levels — Salience (are we known, in what
situations) → Performance/Imagery (what do we deliver, what does following us say about
someone) → Judgments/Feelings (credible? superior? warmth?) → Resonance (active loyalty,
community, attachment). Each level requires the one below it — no Resonance without
Salience. [CANON: Keller, "Conceptualizing, Measuring, and Managing Customer-Based Brand
Equity," *Journal of Marketing*, 1993; *Strategic Brand Management*, textbook editions
since] [VERIFIED-ONLINE:
https://umbrex.com/resources/frameworks/marketing-frameworks/keller-customer-based-brand-equity-cbbe-pyramid/,
2026-07-31].

**Aaker's five dimensions:** brand loyalty, brand awareness, perceived quality, brand
associations, proprietary assets (trademarks, distinctive assets in Aaker's older
vocabulary). [CANON: Aaker, *Managing Brand Equity*, 1991] [VERIFIED-ONLINE:
https://umbrex.com/resources/frameworks/marketing-frameworks/aaker-brand-equity-model/,
2026-07-31].

**How the Brand Office uses these:** shared vocabulary for diagnosing *where* a brand-health
question sits, never a rubric to average into a score. Practical mapping for Tangent,
pre-launch: we are entirely below Salience on Keller's pyramid (the account doesn't exist
yet) — everything before ~1K followers is Salience-building work. Judgments/Feelings and
Resonance work (trust ledger, community rituals — `community-trust.md`) becomes a live
concern only once there's a real audience to hold judgments. Demanding Resonance-tier
tactics (loyalty programs, community rituals) at 0 followers is a category error the
Brand Office should catch at G3 or `/consult`.

## 6. Consistency compounds

**Mere exposure + fluency:** repeated exposure to a stimulus — a face, a format, a phrase,
a visual motif — increases liking even without conscious recognition; one of the most
replicated findings in social psychology. [CANON: Zajonc, *Journal of Personality and
Social Psychology Monograph Supplement*, 1968; Kunst-Wilson & Zajonc, 1980; Bornstein's
1989 meta-analysis of 200+ studies] [REPO: research/03_virality_science.md §2.4.2].
Already operationalized in `knowledge/brand/tangent-voice.md`'s naming-convention rule (Claude named
consistently, the critic a recurring named character) — this file supplies the research
citation; that file has the execution rule.

**Long-term brand building vs. short-term activation — the 60/40 rule:** analysis of
~1,000 IPA Databank case studies found brands splitting effort roughly 60% long-term
brand-building / 40% short-term activation achieved the best long-run outcomes (share,
pricing power, lower acquisition cost); activation produces a sharp, short-lived uplift,
brand-building a slower, compounding one. [CANON: Binet & Field, *The Long and the Short of
It*, IPA, 2013] [VERIFIED-ONLINE: https://growthmethod.com/long-and-short/, 2026-07-31].

**[CONTESTED] caveats, kept visible:** the IPA Databank is a corpus of *award-entered*
case studies — agencies chose to submit and write these up to win effectiveness awards, a
self-selected sample, not a peer-reviewed random sample of all advertising; this is a real
methodological limitation practitioners raise. The 60/40 ratio is itself an aggregated
average, not a fixed law — Binet & Field's later work adjusts it by category, purchase
frequency, and brand maturity. **[EXTRAPOLATION], flagged plainly:** the underlying
research is about paid-media budget allocation for established brands; Tangent has no
paid-media budget (never-buy-reach, `CLAUDE.md` non-negotiable 3) and is pre-launch, so
there is no "budget" to split 60/40. The transferable principle, not the ratio: not every
piece of content should be short-term-activation-shaped (funnel CTAs, keyword-DM asks).
HERO/WORKHORSE/FILLER tier discipline (`content-architecture.md`) already encodes a
version of "most output builds the brand, a minority asks for something" — that's the
60/40 rule's spirit applied to content mix rather than ad spend.
