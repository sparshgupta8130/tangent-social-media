# Scripting & Voice — Operational Doctrine

Load-bearing for `/script` and G1/G2 lens 4 (CLARITY & ONE-IDEA) and the tone component of
lens 5 (CRAFT & AESTHETICS). Governs what gets said, written on-screen, and captioned.

## One-idea discipline

Cramming multiple takeaways into a 15–60s video dilutes both clarity and shareability versus
one clean takeaway [RESEARCH-BACKED direction / PRACTITIONER LORE framing: research/03 §3.2]. A
listicle is not an exception — the "one idea" is the list itself (e.g., "5 mistakes"), so each
item must serve one unifying frame, not wander across unrelated ideas. Operational test: can the
video's core takeaway be stated in one sentence before scripting the beats? If not, split it or
cut scope until it can.

## Sound-off legibility

69% of viewers watch social video with sound off in public, 25% even in private; 80% say
captions make them more likely to watch to completion, and 80% of caption users have no hearing
impairment — captions are a viewing-preference default, not an accessibility add-on
[INDUSTRY DATA: Verizon Media & Publicis Media 2019, n=5,616, via research/03 §3.3.1].
**Caption rule:** the core message must be legible from on-screen text + visuals alone, sound
off — this is lens 4's sound-off test verbatim. If the payoff only lands through narration with
no matching on-screen text, the script fails clarity regardless of how good the audio is.

## On-screen text style — the 35+ tone standard

LENS-1 (`strategy/BETS.md`): jargon-free, calm-confident tone unlocks the YT-majority 35+
audience [RESEARCH-BACKED: research/04 §1 — 35+ is 51.9% of YT's ad-reachable audience] and the
gender-balanced-but-confidence-gapped creative-AI audience [research/04 §2] without costing
reach among the young — "the awe must survive the calm" (`strategy/BETS.md` LENS-1).

**Banned-words starter list** (hype vocabulary, youth-coded slang — cut on sight from on-screen
text and captions): *insane, mind-blowing, you won't believe, game-changer, crazy, unreal,
literally broke the internet, this changes everything, no cap, obsessed, hits different, the
internet is losing it.*

**Preferred register:** specific, concrete, craft vocabulary — name the tool, the setting, the
adjustment, the number. "Lifted the shadows two stops and pulled the color temp back 400K" beats
"this AI edit is INSANE." Precision reads as confidence; hype vocabulary reads as compensating
for a weak payoff — and the payoff is supposed to be doing the work
(`craft/hooks.md` — sell the image, not the AI).

## VO guidelines (when used)

Voiceover is a `[GEN]` asset per SYSTEM_DESIGN §1's provider tags when it's TTS — every TTS VO
follows the disclosure rule in `knowledge/production/genvideo-playbook.md` (state AI-generated
narration on-screen or in caption; never pass synthetic voice as human). Prefer no VO over a
generic AI-narrator register: on-screen text plus real UI sound (clicks, slider drags, shutter)
carries the demo-in-motion grammar better than narration in most of our formats
(`craft/structure-and-retention.md`).

## Caption (text-post) structure

- **First-~125-characters rule:** Instagram truncates captions behind a "more" tap at roughly
  this length — the hook's textual payoff (or its written echo) must live inside that window,
  not after it [PRACTITIONER LORE — platform UI behavior, not independently verified in the
  research corpus; treat the exact character count as approximate].
- **Disclosure line placement:** the AI-involvement disclosure (`audience/positioning.md`) goes
  in the caption body, not buried in a hashtag block — visible without a "more" tap wherever
  disclosure is load-bearing to how the post reads, which per CLAUDE.md non-negotiable #2 is
  always.
- The keyword-DM CTA (`platform/instagram.md`) sits on its own short line, separate from the
  hook line, so truncation never hides the CTA either.

## Quotable-beat engineering — the critic as our native quotable line

The corpus's best "send this to ___" moments are single, sharp, memorable lines
(STEPPS Social Currency, [RESEARCH-BACKED: research/03 §2.2]). Tangent's structural advantage:
the critic's on-screen verdicts are genuine, unscripted, and brutal — engineer at least one
critic one-liner per HERO/WORKHORSE piece as the designed quotable beat (a rejection, a blunt
"the shadows are lying to you," a flat "SHIP"). This is differentiation nobody else in the
corpus has — no other creator studied shows an adversarial internal critique loop on-screen
(`craft/hooks.md` — critic-rejection moment as a hook surface).

## Series numbering conventions

Number every entry in a wrapper series plainly, on-screen and in the caption ("Edit Log 04,"
not a bare title) — the numbering itself, not narrative compounding, is what converts one-time
viewers into followers (`knowledge/brand/content-architecture.md`; series mechanics honest read:
[CORPUS: pattern-library §2.3]). Keep numbering consistent across platforms (same episode
number on IG and YT) so cross-platform viewers recognize the same wrapper, and never renumber
or restart a series once it's live — that resets the mere-exposure compounding the numbering
exists to build (`knowledge/brand/tangent-voice.md`).

## Worked example — banned-register rewrite

Bad (hype register, fails the 35+ tone standard and reads as compensating for a weak payoff):
*"This AI edit is INSANE — you won't believe what happened next!!"*
Good (specific, concrete, craft vocabulary — the payoff carries the line):
*"Claude pulled the highlights back two stops and rebuilt the sky in nine passes. The critic
rejected the first six."*
The rewrite test: if removing every banned word leaves the sentence with nothing to say, the
script has a payoff problem, not a wording problem — fix the concept, not just the copy.

## Operational check (run at script review, before G1)

1. Does every on-screen text card avoid the banned-words list above?
2. Is there at least one exact, concrete detail (a setting, a number, a named tool/panel) in
   the first on-screen text card, rather than a vague superlative?
3. If VO/TTS is used, is the disclosure line present in the same script pass, not added later?
4. Does the caption's first ~125 characters contain the hook's textual echo, not just the
   disclosure or hashtags?
5. If this is a series entry, does the on-screen number match the caption number and the
   previous episode's numbering scheme exactly?
