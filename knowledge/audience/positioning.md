## The story

An engineer is teaching an AI taste — his camera, his code, his taste. Photographs first; video,
music, and apps are later seasons of the same multi-year arc, not separate gimmicks
[STRATEGY §2]. The pipeline is real and filmable: Claude driving Lightroom through a browser,
gated by a critic that actually rejects work. That is the moat — nobody in the 18-dossier corpus
has this pipeline, and every frame of it is independently verifiable by the audience
[STRATEGY §8].

**The structural rule:** every documented flashpoint targeted **undisclosed** generation,
**fully-autonomous** generation presented as unmediated art, or use of **other people's**
work/photos without consent. **None** targeted disclosed, human-curated, tool-assisted craft
using the creator's own material [research/04 §5]. This is not a hope — it is the
evidence-grounded basis for the entire positioning.

## Disclosure doctrine

Say it loudly and first — disclosure is a brand asset here, not a legal chore
(`STRATEGY.md` §2).

- **Bio line** (tangentideas.ai): states plainly that an AI agent edits, under human direction
  and a critique gate, using the owner's own photographs. Draft phrasing: *"An engineer teaching
  an AI taste. Every edit: Claude-driven, human-graded, my own raws."*
- **Caption line:** every post states the AI's role in plain language, inside the visible
  (pre-"more"-tap) portion of the caption where load-bearing (`craft/scripting-voice.md`
  first-125-char rule). Example: *"Claude drove every adjustment here — I set the brief, the
  critic rejected two passes before this one shipped."*
- **On-screen moment:** at least one visible beat per video showing the human gate in action —
  the critic's verdict, a rejected pass, the brief being set. Not a disclaimer bolted on; it is
  the content (`craft/hooks.md` — critic-rejection moment as a hook surface).
- Gen-video/TTS assets carry the stricter disclosure rule in
  `knowledge/production/genvideo-playbook.md` — always visibly flagged as generated, never
  passed as captured footage or a human voice.

## The never-list (constitutional, from CLAUDE.md)

Own work only — Sparsh's raws, our pipeline, our rights; never other people's images or IP.
Disclosure-first, always. Never buy reach — no boosts, ever (three corpus autopsies: kallaway's
self-funded spikes, handlytech's sponsor flops, tiffintech's paid-amplification economics —
`virality/laws.md` Law 0.1). No fabricated numbers. Sell the image, not the AI. Real deliverables
only behind any keyword gate. Critic verdicts are binding. Filler never replaces hero quality.
These do not get relitigated per-post — a session may only deviate by writing the exception down
in the item folder with the critic's agreement (CLAUDE.md).

## Geographic asymmetry

The backlash is almost entirely a US/Western-creative-class phenomenon — Cara, ArtStation,
SDCC/FanX, SAG-AFTRA, and Meta's Muse retreat are all US-market events. India remains net-
optimistic about AI despite rising concern; Southeast Asia is the most AI-optimistic region
measured anywhere in the underlying research [RESEARCH-BACKED: research/04 §5]. Do not assume
backlash-defensive framing is equally load-bearing everywhere our audience sits — but do not
relax the never-list regionally either; the rules are constitutional, not market-conditional.

## Engage-with-craft protocol (responding to anti-AI comments)

Never defensive, never provoked. When a comment challenges the AI's role:
1. Show the human gate, concretely — name the specific brief/rejection/adjustment a human made,
   don't assert "there's a human involved" abstractly.
2. Invite scrutiny of process rather than resisting it — offer to show more, not less (e.g.,
   "happy to post the reject reel if that's useful").
3. Never argue the philosophy of AI-in-art in the comments; stay anchored to this specific
   piece's specific, checkable process.
4. If the comment is bad-faith/hostile rather than a genuine objection, do not engage further —
   silence is not weakness here; escalation is the only losing move.

This protocol exists because the audience most likely to raise this (persona (d),
`audience/personas.md`) is reachable only through calm, disclosed craft, and is actively
alienated by defensiveness or debate-club energy.

**Example reply (engage-with-craft, not defensive):** *"Fair to ask — here's exactly what
happened: I shot this on [date], set the brief for the sky rebuild, and the critic rejected the
first pass for flattening the clouds. Happy to post the reject if useful."* Contrast with what
we never say: *"It's not fully AI, don't worry"* (vague, unfalsifiable) or *"Actually AI art is
totally valid, you're just gatekeeping"* (defensive, provokes rather than de-escalates).

## Why this matters more for us than for most of the corpus

BET-3 (`strategy/BETS.md`) — agentic editing as visible spectacle — is explicitly the
differentiation play and the highest-backlash-adjacent bet in our portfolio: showing an agent
*actively editing* photographs sits closer to the ArtStation/Cara flashpoint pattern than any
tool-demo/automation content in the corpus, none of which touches photography or claims of
authorship over an image. This is exactly why disclosure is doctrine, not decoration, for every
BET-3 post specifically — the on-screen human-gate moment (above) is not optional polish on
this format, it is the risk mitigation itself [research/04 §5 backlash map; STRATEGY §2].
