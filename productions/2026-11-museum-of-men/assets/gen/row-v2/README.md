## How the picks were chosen

I checked every candidate at full width and in zoomed crops of the far half of the row, where the errors live.

- **Round 1 (8 images):** no candidate had exactly 7 correct objects. The failures were extra or empty plinths, a duplicated T-shirt or chappals, the remote split into a "sleeve" and a "remote" on two plinths, all-blue chappals instead of white footbeds, and black phone screens. All rejected.
- **Round 2 (8 images):** `9x16-c3` is the first clean one: 7 plinths and 7 objects in order. It has white-footbed blue-strap chappals, a brass, silver and iron key ring, a navy tee with a photo print, the remote inside a yellowed sleeve, and a phone with a blank light-grey screen. The other 9:16 candidates had empty or extra plinths, or a light grey tee with a red/blue print. `4x5-c1` also had 7 objects, but the tee print had lettering (a "college" style word), and its flat light lost the moody tone. Rejected. The other 4:5 candidates had duplicates or empty plinths.
- **Round 3 (4:5 only, 4 images):** using the 9:16 pick as the reference kept the objects locked. `c3` matches the 9:16 pick most closely: 7 plinths, the same keys including the long iron key, the same chappals, tee, remote and phone. `c4` had an empty extra plinth. `c2` had all-blue chappals. `c1` was close, but its keys lacked the iron key.

Both picks: the top ~25-30% is plain wall (the 4:5 pick's farthest phone sits at about 30% height), the light is dark and warm with a pool on each plinth, and there is no readable text at zoom. Six rounds were run in total across both aspects: 2 for 9:16 and 3 for 4:5, within the 3-round limit per aspect.

## Honest differences that remain (see `comparison.jpg`)

The objects are the same kind, colour and order as the exhibits and read as the same things at feed size. They are **not pixel-identical**, and a close look shows:

1. **Drawer:** the same old wooden drawer with a knob. It holds fewer, looser cables than exhibit 1's dense tangle.
2. **Phone box:** the biggest miss. It is white with a leaning lid, as in the exhibit. But it is clean and sleek with a small dark camera-hole dot on the lid, while exhibit 2 is scuffed and yellowed at the edges with a visible moulded tray.
3. **Keys:** a steel ring with brass, silver and a long dark iron key, the right mix. There are about 6 keys against the exhibit's 9, and the black-headed scooter key is not clear.
4. **Chappals:** a close match: white footbeds, blue straps, toes forward. The safety pin is not visible at this scale.
5. **T-shirt:** the right navy colour, folded, with a photo print. The print is a pale landscape-like photo, not exhibit 5's brownish figure print.
6. **Remote:** a close match: a dark remote inside a yellowed, creased clear sleeve.
7. **Phone:** black, on a clear stand, blank light-grey screen. It has a top and bottom bezel (older style), while exhibit 7 is a full-screen notch phone.

If the phone box (2) or tee print (5) mismatch matters at the size it is used, the fastest fix is a local inpaint of that one plinth with the exhibit image as reference, not another full regeneration.
