## Part 1. Where I disagree with the brief

1. **"Most of what goes viral is photoreal, so we must go photoreal."** That is partly true for
   views and wrong for praise.
   - The two biggest AI moments of 2025 were Ghibli (stylised, not photoreal) and the Nano Banana
     saree trend, which worked because it showed *your own face* [F§5].
   - The most-viewed photoreal clip (the fake bunny cam) got its reach by fooling people.
   - Polished photoreal brand ads (Coca-Cola, McDonald's NL) got "soulless" backlash [F§5].
   - So what wins is **specific, personal and warm**. Photoreal is a tool, not the strategy.
   - Use photoreal where it adds something: a wildlife-documentary parody, your own face aged,
     restored family photos.
2. **"Emotional: men's hidden stress".** Purely sad posts are the weakest choice for shares.
   - Research links sadness to *less* sharing; amusement and awe drive it [F§4].
   - Indian IMD comment sections turn lectures and grievances into gender wars [F§3].
   - So **do not drop the emotion; put it last.** Make them laugh and recognise someone, then
     land one kind, true line. Every strong idea below has that shape.
3. **"Men will be men".** That phrase is Imperial Blue whisky's ad tagline, running since about
   2000 [F§3]. We would be quoting an alcohol brand.
   - The *spirit* (affectionate "that's so him") is great. We write our own line.
4. **Jensen Huang faceswaps.** Your premise is correct: he resigned on 12 Nov and parody clips are
   everywhere [F§6]. I still recommend we don't touch it. See idea 15 for the reasons.
   - The *mechanic* behind it (faceswap comedy) is worth stealing, done on your own face.
5. **A rule you'll be asked to waive.** `knowledge/production/genvideo-playbook.md` §2 says
   generated video is **b-roll only, never the subject**.
   - Ideas 2, 3, 8, 9 and 14 (and 4, if made with Veo) make generated video the main content.
   - That is allowed only as a written exception in the item folder that the critic agrees with
     (CLAUDE.md, last line).
   - Alternatively, the rule gets updated at a weekly review.
   - If you want the "more sophisticated generation" direction long-term, change the playbook
     rather than waiving it post by post.

## Part 3. All ideas

Format for each: **what it is**, **platforms**, **how to make it**, **why it could work**,
**risks**, **effort**.

Effort scale:
- **S**: a few hours.
- **M**: about a day.
- **L**: 2-4 days.
- **XL**: a week or more.

### A. Funny first, warm last (the evidence-favoured shape)

**1. The Museum of Men** ⭐
- **What:** a gallery exhibition of the things every man has.
  - Each one is shot like a museum piece: white space, a spotlight, and a Fraunces plaque.
  - "Exhibit 3: The Cable Drawer. Holds a charger for a phone sold in 2011. Will not be thrown out."
  - "Exhibit 5: The Bag of Bags." (Check this isn't more of a mom thing.)
  - "Exhibit 7: The Screwdriver Set. Last used 2019. Still 'very useful'."
  - "Exhibit 9: The 'Important Documents' Folder."
  - "Exhibit 11: The AC remote, in its original plastic cover."
  - Last exhibit: **His phone.** "He'll never say he misses you. He'll say 'all good?' Call him today."
- **Platforms:**
  - IG carousel (10 slides) or a slow-push reel.
  - LinkedIn document post: a work version, "The Museum of the Working Man", with the laptop bag,
    the tiffin and the 11 pm "quick call".
  - YT Short.
  - Could also live as a real page on tangent.gallery.
- **How to make it:**
  - Best: you photograph the real objects at your dad's or your own place. Your raws, edited by
    the Claude/Lightroom pipeline. Fully on-pillar.
  - Faster: Nano Banana Pro still lifes, disclosed.
  - Plaques built in code, the same way as the Panditji carousel builds.
- **Why it could work:**
  - Recognition humour about habits, not incompetence [F§3 ASA].
  - Each slide says "that's my dad", which means sends [F§4].
  - The gallery look is our own distinctive asset.
- **Risks:**
  - Objects must feel Indian-specific, not generic Western dad.
  - Mild stereotype risk.
- **Effort:** S (generated) / M (photographed).

**2. I played every man in my family** ⭐ (needs your face approval)
- **What:** one selfie, aged and restyled into Papa, Dadaji, Chachu, Bhaiya and yourself. Each does
  his signature move:
  - Papa switches off the lights the moment you leave a room.
  - Dadaji guards the newspaper.
  - Chachu forwards a "Good Morning" image at 5:02 am.
  - Bhaiya says "it was on my way" after driving 40 km.
  - Final shot: all five at one dining table. None says "I love you". One asks "khana khaya?".
    Caption beat: "That's how they say it."
- **Platforms:** IG reel (hero), YT Short, and a LinkedIn clip with a founder note.
- **How to make it:**
  - Nano Banana Pro for consistent aged portraits from your selfie (up to 5 consistent people [F§5]).
  - Veo 3.1 Ingredients-to-Video using those portraits as reference images.
  - Optional: your own cloned voice for each man's one line (ElevenLabs). Cloning your own voice
    is exempt from YouTube's disclosure rule, but we disclose anyway.
  - Show the raw selfie-to-portrait step as the process proof.
- **Why it could work:**
  - It is the Nano Banana insight: a real person's own face, personal and culturally specific.
  - It is the Jensen trend's mechanic with full consent.
- **Risks:**
  - The uncanny valley.
  - Five-person consistency across video is hard and will take many generations (the Kalshi ad
    needed 300-400 for 15 clips [F§5]).
  - Your face is a hard approval point.
- **Effort:** L.

**3. Wildlife documentary: The Indian Man in his natural habitat**
- **What:** a nature-documentary parody with a hushed narrator.
  - "Here, the male gathers at the open bonnet of a stranger's car. He does not know cars. He must
    still be present."
  - "At the wedding buffet, the uncle has scouted the paneer counter in advance."
  - "Observe the father defending the AC remote, set at 24 degrees."
  - Warm close: "Rarely observed: the male saying he is tired. Commonly observed: making sure
    everyone got home. Check on yours."
- **Platforms:** IG reel, YT Short, and a longer 2-3 min YT cut.
- **How to make it:**
  - Veo 3.1 photoreal "wildlife" footage with native ambient audio.
  - Your own voice as narrator (never a clone of a famous narrator).
- **Why it could work:**
  - An established comedy genre.
  - The obvious staging makes disclosure natural, not a confession.
  - It shows off photoreal generation.
- **Risks:**
  - Stereotyping. The ASA lesson: affection, not "men are useless" [F§3].
  - Indian viewers will catch wrong cultural details (the Mahabharat lesson [F§5]).
- **Effort:** L.

**4. "It was on my way": the Indian man's love language**
- **What:** rapid vignettes of love disguised as logistics. Each shows a big act of care, captioned
  with what he *says*:
  - Drove 40 km to the station: "It was on my way."
  - Fixed your WiFi at midnight: "Just restart it."
  - Paid your fees quietly: "Don't worry about it."
  - Checked your tyre pressure: "Theek hai."
  - Close: "Men don't always say it. Learn to hear it."
- **Platforms:** IG reel or carousel, YT Short.
- **How to make it:** text-led carousel (S), or Veo vignettes (M-L).
- **Why it could work:** pure "that's my dad / bhai" recognition, with a high send pull.
- **Risks:** low.
- **Effort:** S to L.

**5. The unsent message**
- **What:** a phone screen.
  - A man types "Bro, I've been having a really hard time lately…", deletes it, and sends
    "all good bro 👍".
  - His friend replies "👍".
  - Then rewind: the friend instead writes "Sure? Call kar."
  - Close: "This Men's Day, don't accept the first 'all good'."
- **Platforms:** IG reel, YT Short. Very strong for LinkedIn as a native video.
- **How to make it:**
  - The chat UI drawn in code, like the Panditji renders.
  - Label it a dramatisation. Our "real UI" rule is about tool demos; this is plainly fiction.
- **Why it could work:**
  - Simple, true and actionable: viewers forward it to a friend.
  - The JED research says men signal distress through jokes and short replies [F§4].
- **Risks:**
  - Sadder in tone. Needs the warm, actionable turn.
  - Add a helpline in the caption (safe messaging [F§2]).
- **Effort:** S.

**6. Men's love-language taxonomy**
- **What:** an illustrated "field chart":
  - Words of affirmation: "Hmm."
  - Acts of service: refills your water bottle before your trip.
  - Quality time: sitting silently in the same room, watching cricket.
  - Gifts: forwards you a 14-minute YouTube video.
  - Physical touch: one firm pat on the back.
- **Platforms:** IG carousel, LinkedIn document post.
- **Effort:** S.
- **Risks:** low. It is close to existing "love languages" memes, so it is less original.

### B. Emotional, done with care

**7. Dadaji's photo, finished** (the most on-pillar idea)
- **What:** you scan an old family photo of the men in your family, maybe the only photo your
  grandfather has.
  - The Claude/Lightroom pipeline restores it on camera: demo-in-motion, format (a).
  - The critic rejects one over-smooth pass.
  - Then the final print.
  - Optional: a gentle, disclosed Veo animation of a smile or blink.
  - Line: "My grandfather has one photo of himself. I spent a week making it the photo he deserved."
- **Platforms:** IG reel, YT Short, and a YT long-form "restoring three generations of men in my
  family", which fits BET-4.
- **How to make it:** your family's photo (needs family consent) and our existing pipeline.
- **Why it could work:**
  - It is literally the brand: machine craft, human taste, own material.
  - Awe plus warmth.
- **Risks:**
  - Animating late relatives can feel uncanny or upsetting to family. Make the restoration the
    hero and the animation optional.
  - How audiences receive family-photo animation is UNKNOWN [F gaps].
- **Effort:** M-L.

**8. Me at 70 talks to me at 30** (needs your face and voice approval)
- **What:** an AI-aged you, photoreal with your own cloned voice, gives your current self advice
  about being a man:
  - "Go to the doctor when it hurts."
  - "Call your father on a normal day, not just birthdays."
  - "Your friends needed you to ask twice."
  - Ends on present-you at the desk, picking up the phone.
- **Platforms:** IG reel, YT Short. Strong LinkedIn video with an engineer-founder note.
- **How to make it:**
  - Nano Banana Pro for aging.
  - Veo 3.1 or a lip-sync tool for the talking head.
  - ElevenLabs clone of your own voice.
- **Why it could work:** personal, specific, and a sophisticated showcase.
- **Risks:**
  - The uncanny valley.
  - Could read as self-indulgent if the advice is generic. It needs real, specific lines from you.
- **Effort:** M-L.

**9. The invisible weight**
- **What:** a photoreal man going about his day. What he carries slowly becomes visible:
  - EMI papers on his back;
  - his parents' medicine strip in his pocket;
  - a fees receipt;
  - his kid's school bag.
  - Someone asks "how are you?" He says "badhiya". The weight disappears from view, but not for him.
- **Platforms:** IG reel, YT Short.
- **How to make it:** Veo 3.1, Frames-to-Video.
- **Risks:**
  - The sadness penalty [F§4].
  - Closest to the "soulless AI ad" register that failed for Coke and McDonald's [F§5].
- **Effort:** L.
- **My rating:** lowest of the emotional set.

**10. Portraits: men in my life** (photography-first)
- **What:** you photograph 6-8 real men with their consent: your dad, a friend, the
  presswallah, the watchman, a colleague.
  - The pipeline edits the portraits.
  - Each gets one plain line about what he carries, in his own words, from a 2-minute chat.
- **Platforms:** IG carousel, a tangent.gallery exhibition, a LinkedIn carousel.
- **Why it could work:** real faces and real words, the opposite of slop.
- **Risks:** consent forms, and time.
- **Effort:** XL.

### C. On-thesis: AI's idea of a man vs. a human's

**11. "I asked AI to show me a successful Indian man"** ⭐ (LinkedIn lead, also IG)
- **What:** run the same prompt through the image models.
  - Show the defaults: suits, skyline, serious face, rich, stressed. The "Indian men created using
    stereotypes and AI" meme from 2022 shows this lands [F§3].
  - Then your counter-picks: your dad at the scooter, a friend who left a job for his mental health.
  - Line: "AI learned what a successful man looks like from ads. I'd rather show it the men I know."
- **Platforms:** LinkedIn (insight post, no "agree?" bait), IG carousel.
- **Why it could work:**
  - It is our thesis, "machine craft, human taste", on screen.
  - It is insight-led, the kind of post LinkedIn's ranking rewards [F§7].
- **Risks:**
  - Must be honest about what each model actually produced: screenshots, no cherry-picking.
- **Effort:** S-M.

**12. Making-of: "My AI critic rejected 6 of my Men's Day jokes"**
- **What:** a behind-the-scenes cut of whichever piece we make.
  - Story-lead drafts jokes; the critic kills the mean ones ("this joke is about him being useless,
    not about him being loving"). The finished piece is the payoff.
- **Platforms:** YT long-form or Short, LinkedIn.
- **Why it could work:** it shows the human gate, which our disclosure doctrine says is the content
  itself.
- **Effort:** M, on top of the main piece.

**13. Dad-text translator (a tiny app)**
- **What:** a web toy on tangent.gallery. You type what you feel ("I'm really proud of you and I
  miss you"); it returns how an Indian dad would text it ("Ok. 👍 Call when free.").
  - Built with Claude.
  - Comment "DAD" to get the link: a real, working deliverable, so it passes our engagement-bait
    rule.
- **Platforms:** IG reel demo plus DM funnel (BET-2 data), LinkedIn "I built this in an evening".
- **Risks:** it must actually be good; a thin toy breaks trust [personas (c)].
- **Effort:** M.

### D. Faceswap, consent-based

**14. Your friends as action heroes** (with written consent)
- **What:** your real friend group, faceswapped into a heist-movie trailer about the most
  heroic thing men do: splitting a restaurant bill fairly.
- **Platforms:** IG reel.
- **Risks:** you need every friend's written consent, and consent tools leak [F§6].
- **Effort:** L.
- **My rating:** fun, but weaker on IMD meaning than #2.

**15. Jensen Huang faceswap: recommend NO**
- **The premise is real** [F§6], but:
  - **(a)** It breaks non-negotiable #1: another person's likeness without consent.
  - **(b)** Part of this exact wave is NewsGuard-flagged, China-linked disinformation. Our clip would
    sit in that feed and could be mistaken for it.
  - **(c)** The story involves an *alleged*, unproven espionage claim. A "funny" clip can imply
    facts, and defamation is the realistic exposure [F§6].
  - **(d)** India's 2026 IT Rules and the Delhi HC's run of personality-rights orders make
    real-person deepfakes a live enforcement area. The parody carve-out is untested [F§6].
  - **(e)** Making fun of a man's affair scandal *on Men's Day* is tonally the opposite of what this
    day is for.
  - **(f)** The news broke on 10-12 Nov. Our 0-4-day window (virality Law 8) has passed.
- Ideas #2 and #14 keep the mechanic and drop all six problems.

## Part 5. Rules every option must follow

- **Disclosure** on screen and in the caption, in plain words. It is now also an Indian legal
  requirement for realistic synthetic media [F§5].
- **The joke is about love or quirks, never incompetence.** No lecture. No "why no men's day"
  grievance. No comparison with Women's Day [F§3].
- **Facts:**
  - Any suicide statistic only after primary verification, with a helpline [F§2].
  - Never present UK numbers as Indian.
- **Plain first** (CLAUDE.md #10): a Message Brief, `clarity_lint`, then one `/cold-read` before you
  see the final post.
- **Brand gate:** this touches gender and mental health, so its Brand-Touch Score is likely 3 or
  more and `/consult` (audience-advocate) is mandatory before scripting.
- **Your approvals:** ideas #2 and #8 use your face and voice, and #7 and #10 use your family and
  real people. Each needs your explicit approval, plus their consent.
