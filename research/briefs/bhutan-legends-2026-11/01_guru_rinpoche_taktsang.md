## 1. Summary
1. The temple at Taktsang was founded 1692 by Gyalse Tenzin Rabgye (4th Druk Desi) and basically completed 1694. Guru Rinpoche did not found a monastery there; tradition says he practised in the cave and subdued spirits there [S1, S2].
2. The "demon" is better rendered as local gods, spirits and demons "bound to serve the dharma" (Dowman's translation of the Yeshe Tsogyal biography, quoted in S3). The Taktsang local deity (Sengye Samdrup) is described by Kinga as a local deity, not a villain.
3. "Rudra form" is INCORRECT. Every Taktsang source opened says Dorje Drolo (Dorje Trollo). No opened source links Taktsang to Rudra. The Rudra subjugation is a separate, real myth in Nyingma literature [S9].
4. The tigress is disputed even in Bhutanese scholarship: Yeshe Tsogyal (popular), Tashi Khyidren (Dowman's biography; Karma Phuntsho via Kinga), Monmo Tashi Khyidren daughter of Sendha Gyalp (a minority view), Mandarava (Himalayan Art item 731). Himalayan Art Resources itself says Yeshe Tsogyal on a different page.
5. Guru Rinpoche came to Bhutan first via Bumthang (Sindhu Raja / Shelging Karpo) by tradition; Kinga: "He did not visit Taktshang at that time." Buddhism had earlier footholds (Songtsen Gampo temples, 7th c., per secondary sources only).

## 3. The story as most accepted in Bhutan (about 600 words)

[TRADITION] Guru Rinpoche, "Precious Guru", is the name Bhutanese use for Padmasambhava, the "Lotus-Born" tantric master from Oddiyana who in the 8th century is believed to have helped establish Buddhism across the Himalaya.[S5, S10] Bhutanese accounts say he first came to Bumthang, invited by king Sindhu Raja (Sendha Gyalp), who was ill after his life force was taken by the local deity Shelging Karpo. The Guru subdued the deity, made him a protector of the dharma, and left his body imprint in the cave at Kurjey.[S3, S10, S16 (lead)]

[TRADITION] Taktsang is counted as one of the "thirteen Tiger's Dens" where he appeared as the wrathful Dorje Drolo to subdue spirits.[S7, S8] The popular telling: the Guru, in that form, flew from Tibet or Senge Dzong on a tigress. Pilgrims are usually told the tigress was his consort Yeshe Tsogyal.[S3, S14] The Bhutanese scholar Sonam Kinga, reading Yeshe Tsogyal's own biography as translated by Dowman, reports a different picture: the Guru became Dorje Trollo, Yeshe Tsogyal became Ekajati, and the Bhutanese girl Tashi Khyidren became the tigress. A blue-black emanation "flew directly to Paro Taktsang, and there he subjugated gods, demons... and bound them to serve the dharma."[S3]

[TRADITION] The Guru is said to have hidden treasures (terma) at Taktsang and blessed an image of Dorje Trollo: "This is the place of Guru's Mind."[S3 quoting Dowman]. A cave prayer sign calls the cave "Sengye Samdrup" and lists what Dorje Drolo did there: "Subduing vicious heretics, demons and gnomes."[S3]

[HISTORY] Later teachers practised at the cliffs. The Rubin essay says the caves received treasure texts, which later treasure-revealers recovered.[S2] Wikipedia (lead; citing Pommaret 2006) adds Langchen Pelkyi Singye in 853 (naming the cave Pelphug), and from the 11th century Milarepa, Pha Dampa Sangye, Machig Labdron and Thangtong Gyalpo.[S10b] Not independently verified.

[HISTORY] In the Tibetan war of 1644/46, Zhabdrung Ngawang Namgyel and his Nyingma teacher Rigdzin Nyingpo performed rituals at Taktsang. In a vision the local deity appeared "in the form of a black man" and offered Taktsang to him.[S1] The Zhabdrung never built the planned temple.

[HISTORY] In 1692, during Tshechu, Gyalse Tenzin Rabgye, the 4th Druk Desi (1638-96), stood at the cave entrance and ordered the foundation of the "Temple of the Guru with Eight Names" (Guru Tsengye Lhakhang). Work began in the 10th month of 1692 under artisan Drakpa Gyatso (1646-1719) and was basically done by 1694, when he returned for the consecration.[S1] His biographer saw him as a return of Padmasambhava.[S1]

[HISTORY] A fire in 1998 destroyed much of the complex; HAR reports re-consecration in March 2005.[S4, S2] Wikipedia (lead): fire 19 April 1998, one monk died, cost 135 million ngultrum.[S10b]

## 4. Timeline
- 7th c. (627 or 659, sources disagree): Songtsen Gampo temples at Paro (Kyichu) and Bumthang (Jambay) [S12, S13; secondary, tradition-tinged].
- 8th c.: Guru Rinpoche's visits [TRADITION]. First visit dated 746 or 747 (travel/heritage sites [S13, S16]) or c. 810 (Wikipedia [S10]). UNKNOWN which Bhutanese authority is standard.
- 853: Langchen Pelkyi Singye at the cave, Pelphug [LEAD, S10b].
- 1124-1192: Nyangrel Nyima Wozer writes the earliest Padmasambhava biography [S2].
- 1644/46: Zhabdrung at Taktsang [S1].
- 1692: foundation laid at Tshechu; first Paro Tshechu [S1, S2]. 10th month 1692: work begins; 1694: consecration [S1].
- 1951: fire from a villager clearing land; not total loss [S4].
- 19 April 1998: fire [S10b]; March 2005 re-consecration [S4].

## 5. Variants and disagreements
- Tigress identity [S3, S4, S7]: (i) Yeshe Tsogyal (popular; National Geographic [S14]; HAR set 2860 [S4]); (ii) Tashi Khyidren, a Bhutanese girl, daughter of local chief Hangrey/Hamrey (Karma Phuntsho 2013 via Kinga; Dowman's translation of Yeshe Tsogyal's biography); (iii) Monmo Tashi Khyidren/Bumden Tshomo, daughter of Sendha Gyalp of Bumthang (a "few" per Kinga; Kinga notes this event belongs to the first visit when he "did not visit Taktshang"); (iv) Mandarava (HAR item 731 caption). Kinga's own conclusion: "There is no doubt from Yeshe Tsogyel's statement that the tigress... was the Bhutanese girl Tashi Chidren." Kinga calls his article preliminary; the three Taktshangs (Womphu in Tibet, Paro, Kham) have been confused.
- Where the flight began: Senge Dzong in Lhuntse (signboards and Bhutan Times 2008 per Kinga) versus Tibet [S14] versus Kinga: the transformation happened at Womphu Taktsang in Tibet in the Dowman account, not at Paro or Senge Dzong.
- Subdued what: "evil spirits" [S14], "demons" [S2, S8], "gods, demons, wrathful Dakinis... spirits" [S3]. See sensitivities.
- Meditation duration: 3y 3m 3w 3d 3h [S14, S15]; absent in [S1, S2, S3, S4]. Another search result said 3 years only (not opened). UNKNOWN which is the textual source.
- Rudra: no opened source on Taktsang or Dorje Drolo mentions Rudra [S7, S3, S5, S4]. The Rudra myth exists in Nyingma tradition (Heruka subdues Rudra; the "charter myth", search snippet citing Robert Mayer; page not opened). Jamgon Kongtrul's Padmakara life [S9] does not mention Rudra but gives Padmasambhava the form Guru Drakpo ("Wrathful Guru") when binding the spirit Shing Cha Chen and the name Dorje Drakpo Tsal ("Wrathful Vajra Might"). Possible mix-up: Rudra, Drakpo, Drolo. No evidence that the owner's phrase has a textual source; flag as probable mishearing or conflation. Do not tell the owner it is "wrong" more strongly than: no support found.
- Founding year: the lead's "1508, Sonam Gyaltshen" snippet could not be sourced anywhere: UNKNOWN, ignore unless found.
- Dorje Drolo and the eight forms: "last of the Guru's names to be standardized" [S5]; Rubin glossary: "Different texts give varied lists" [S2b].
- Dorje Drolo emanations: Wikipedia (lead, citing Palden Sherab) says 13 emanations for 13 Tiger's Nest caves [S10].

## 7. Iconography
Guru Rinpoche, standard form (Pema Jungne, HAR item 361) [S6]:
- Lotus hat, gift of the King of Zahor, "with silk brocade topped with a half-vajra and vulture feather."
- Right hand: "an upright gold vajra" at the heart. Left hand: "a white skullcup filled with nectar" in the lap.
- Khatvanga rests against the left shoulder. Robes "of different colours reflecting the disciplines of the Vinaya, Bodhisattva and Mantra Vehicles."
- Seated "in a relaxed posture with the right foot extended resting on a lotus cushion" on sun and moon disc above a pink lotus. Consorts: Yeshe Tsogyal (left), Mandarava (right), each with a skull cup.
Dorje Drolo (HAR item 731, Rubin Museum) [S7]:
- "Terrifying in appearance, dark red in colour (maroon)", one face, moustache and beard, three round staring eyes, curly dark hair, upper fangs biting lower lip.
- Right hand: gold vajra raised, "ready to strike." Left: dark blue kila (phurba) of meteorite iron, extended.
- Gold earrings; necklace of severed heads; robes in blue and red in the style of both layman and monk; felt boots.
- Stands, right leg bent, left extended, "atop a fierce tigress - emanation of the consort Mandarava", trampling two bodies; sun disc, orange lotus, orange flames of pristine awareness. Rubin essay: corpulent body "wreathed in flames"; vajra right, purba left [S5].
Note: HAR item 731 names the tigress as Mandarava; HAR set 2860 names Yeshe Tsogyal: a visible variant within the same publisher.

## 8. Sensitivities
- Do not call the Guru's wrathful form a "demon" or "monster." Dorje Drolo is a wrathful manifestation of a Buddhist master; HAR: "wrathful emanation of Guru Rinpoche" [S7]. Wrath is compassion in action; the figures he binds become protectors (Shelging Karpo became a protector [S10/S16 lead]; Taktsang's own deity gave the site to the Zhabdrung [S1]). My own recommendation, not a sourced rule: say "local spirits" or "gods and spirits" bound as protectors, not "demon".
- Do not present the tigress as one fixed identity as fact. Yeshe Tsogyal is the common version; Bhutanese scholarship (Kinga) disputes it. The lead's script should say "the tigress, said by many to be Yeshe Tsogyal."
- Do not say Guru Rinpoche "founded" the monastery or that Taktsang is "1,300 years old." The temple is 1692-94 and was rebuilt after 1998.
- Do not depict the 1998 fire as an accident comedy; one monk died (Wikipedia lead via Crossette NYT 1998; not opened).
- Guru Rinpoche is deeply revered; do not parody, rhyme, or put him in comedy. The owner's "his face/voice" approval gate applies to Sparsh's own likeness, not this; but depiction of the Guru in AI needs care: image rules (sacred iconography has set proportions and attributes). Recommend a Bhutanese cultural reader before release. Needs a Bhutanese reviewer.
- Pronunciation errors read as disrespect: get a native-speaker check.
- The Tiger's Nest is an active place of worship and entry is restricted (not researched; UNKNOWN).

## 9. Memorable sourced details for a 5-minute script
1. The "temple" is a monument to a promise kept: Zhabdrung wanted a temple here in 1644 but never built it; Tenzin Rabgye was a young monk in his entourage and fulfilled the wish in 1692 [S1].
2. The local deity of Taktsang appeared to the Zhabdrung "in the form of a black man" and offered the site, vowing none could ever steal it [S1].
3. Tenzin Rabgye's biographer wrote that he was Guru Rinpoche returned, so one need not feel nostalgic for ancient times [S1].
4. "Tashi Khyidren as our mount, the tigress": the tigress was a Bhutanese girl according to Yeshe Tsogyal's own biography [S3].
5. Miracles at the 1692 Tshechu: no one was hurt on the narrow cliff stepways; "a rain of flowers" appeared in the sky [S1].
6. The cave prayer: "Subduing vicious heretics, demons and gnomes" in the cave of Sengye Samdrup [S3].
7. The cliff itself "has the appearance of Dorje Drolo", said to be common knowledge among Bhutanese (Kinga) [S3].
8. Dorje Drolo was "the last of the Guru's names to be standardized" in the eight [S5].
Meditation "3 years, 3 months, 3 weeks, 3 days and 3 hours" is vivid but only travel-grade sourced [S14, S15]; script should say "tradition says" if used.
