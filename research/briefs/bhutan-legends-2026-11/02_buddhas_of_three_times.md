## 1. Summary
1. The three times are three individual Buddhas of successive ages: past (usually Dipamkara, sometimes Kashyapa), present Shakyamuni, future Maitreya. Not "types". Shared across Tibetan Buddhism, not unique to Bhutan.
2. "Each has its own gesture" is PARTLY true. In Himalayan Art Resources (HAR) convention Shakyamuni touches the earth, but Dipamkara and Maitreya share the same teaching gesture. HAR itself says Dipamkara is sometimes identified "by process of elimination".
3. The altar triad a visitor most often sees in dzongs and big lhakhangs is Shakyamuni with Guru Rinpoche and Zhabdrung (Punakha Kuenrey is the best-sourced named example). It is a different set from the three times. But Guru Rinpoche is the central figure in several temples.
4. Guru Rinpoche is called "Buddha of the three times" in a very well-known prayer, a probable source of confusion.
5. The ONLY sourced Bhutan temple example of a past-present-future triad that I opened is Phurdrup (Kasyapa, Shakyamuni, Maitreya, painted clay, ca. 19th c.), and its past Buddha is Kasyapa, not Dipamkara.

## 3. The explainer as most accepted

**What the three times are.** [TRADITION] Buddhists teach that Buddhas appear one after another across vast ages, not once. Tibetan art groups three of them as "the Buddhas of the three times" (Tibetan dus gsum sangs rgyas, in Lotsawa House's transliteration "dü sum sangyé"). The Rubin glossary says "These three are often represented and worshiped together" [1]. They are individual beings, one per age, not three kinds of Buddha.

**Who.** [TRADITION] Past: Dipamkara (Tibetan mar me mdzad, "Marmedze", written in Tibetan script on HAR item 4064) [2]. Present: Shakyamuni, the Buddha of our age. Future: Maitreya (Tibetan Jampa, byams pa) [3]. Variant: some groupings put Kashyapa as the past Buddha, as in the Bhutanese clay triad at Phurdrup [4]; Wikipedia's article says "Dipankara Buddha (or Kassapa Buddha)" (lead only) [5].

**The story of Dipamkara and Sumedha.** [TRADITION] In the Pali tradition, the Buddha-to-be lives as the ascetic Sumedha. Dipamkara walks a muddy road. Sumedha lays himself across it as a bridge: "Then loosened I my matted hair, And, spreading out upon the mud / My dress of bark and cloak of skin, I laid me down upon my face." Dipamkara foretells his future: "Lo! he, unnumbered cycles hence, / A Buddha in the world shall be... His own name shall be Gotama." [6] Note this is Henry Clarke Warren's 1896 translation of the Pali Nidanakatha, a Theravada text. I did not find a Bhutanese or Tibetan telling; the Tibetan version may differ in detail. UNKNOWN.

**Maitreya.** [TRADITION] Maitreya is the next Buddha after Shakyamuni, "presently residing in the pure land of Tushita", and "the fifth of the thousand buddhas of this fortunate eon" [3]. The Rubin glossary: "the next Buddha to appear will be Maitreya" [7]. Wikipedia lists the eon's first five as Kakusandha, Kanakamuni, Kashyapa, Shakyamuni, Maitreya (lead only) [8]. So "future Buddha" is about the order in one aeon of a thousand.

**In Bhutan.** [HISTORY/TRADITION] Jampa Lhakhang in Bumthang has Jowo Jampa (Maitreya) as its main sanctuary image [9]; oral tradition says Songtsen Gampo founded it in the 7th century [9]. The Rubin essay calls its Maitreya "probably the earliest grand Buddhist clay sculpture made in Bhutan" but says "There are no extant records to identify who made the statues or confirm their seventh-century date" [10]. The idea is not Bhutan-specific; Bhutan has its own clay-sculpture tradition for it.

## 4. What you actually see on a Bhutanese altar

**A. Shakyamuni with Guru Rinpoche and Zhabdrung (most-cited arrangement)**
- Punakha Dzong, Kuenrey: "the main 35-foot image of Buddha Shakyamuni", with 28-foot images of Guru Padmasambhava and Zhabdrung Ngawang Namgyal. Source: raonline.ch (Swiss site; publisher and authority UNKNOWN, treat as medium reliability). https://raonline.ch/pages/bt/visin/bt_punakha02b.html . Which side each stands on: the page puts Guru Rinpoche on the Buddha's right and Zhabdrung on his left, but whether that is the Buddha's right or the viewer's right is not stated. Do not animate left/right until checked on site photos.
- Zangthopelri Lhakhang, Thimphu: statues of Guru Rinpoche, Shakyamuni and Zhabdrung in the main shrine (tour operator page, weak). https://www.adbhutantours.com/sightseeing-in-bhutan/thimphu/zangthopelri-lhakhang/
- Zhemgang Dzong and Druk Wangyal Lhakhang: search snippets describe the same triad, but I could not open the pages (bca.clcs.edu.bt blocked; wur.nl timed out). UNKNOWN until read.
- Daily Bhutan on the three figures: Shakyamuni "is the historical founder of Buddhism"; Guru Rinpoche "introduced Buddhism in Bhutan around the 8th century"; Zhabdrung "unified Bhutan and established Drukpa Kagyu as the state religion" (paraphrase of the page). It does NOT say they form a standard triad. http://www.dailybhutan.com/article/the-most-prominent-buddhist-deities-in-bhutan

**B. Guru Rinpoche central** (so claim c is not universal)
- Tamshing: "Guru Rinpoche with a statue of Jampa (Buddha of the future) and Sakyamuni to its sides." https://www.bookmytour.bt/attractions/tamshing-lhakhang (travel site, weak).
- Buli Lhakhang: ground-floor shrine has Guru Padmasambhava in the centre with Zhabdrung on the right and Vajrasattva on the left; a second-floor altar has Shakyamuni in the centre. https://www.tourmyindia.com/international/bhutan/buli-lhakhang/ (travel site, weak).
- Haa Lhakhang Nagpo: central image a Jowo Shakyamuni; Zhabdrung, Songtsen Gampo and Guru Rinpoche "recently installed". https://bhutanpilgrimage.com/lhakhang-nagpo-the-black-temple-of-haa-valley/ (travel site).

**C. Buddhas of the three times as a set**
- Phurdrup Monastery: painted clay, ca. 19th c., attributed to a sculptor of the Hephu tradition, approx. 122 cm; "Three golden Buddhas wearing saffron and red cloaks seated before mandorlas in niche decorated with murals." https://rubinmuseum.org/projecthimalayanart/related/buddha-kasyapa-buddha-shakyamuni-and-maitreya-the-buddhas-of-the-past-present-and-future/ . The page gives no mudras.
- Thangbi Lhakhang (statues of past, present and future, late 15th c.) and Neyphu Gonpa (Dipankara, Shakyamuni, Maitreya): search snippets and a personal blog only. UNKNOWN until opened.
- Jampa Lhakhang: the Rubin essay says the main image is Maitreya "flanked by sculpted sets of the Eight Great Bodhisattvas" [10]; this is a Maitreya shrine, not a three-times triad.
- Punakha, Kyichu, Trongsa, Tango: no three-times triad found. UNKNOWN.

**D. Other groups**
- Rigsum Gonpo (Manjushri, Vajrapani, Avalokiteshvara): Buli Bar Lhakhang murals, described by THL as "Rigsum Goenpo" (https://mms.thlib.org/media_objects/69469).
- Royal Heritage Museum page describes a Maitreya-centred "trinity" with Manjushri and Vajrapani: "highly venerated and widespread throughout the Tibetan world". https://museums.gov.bt/our-museums/royal-heritage-museum/temples-in-the-royal-heritage-museum . Treat carefully; read through a summariser and not cross-checked.
- Shakyamuni with Shariputra and Maudgalyayana: HAR describes this attendant pair in the Gelug set (setID=5262). Bhutan example: UNKNOWN.
- 16 Arhats at Punakha Kuenrey back wall: snippet only. Tsepame/Amitayus and Chenrezig as altar figures: Zhemgang snippet (Amitayus on altar), otherwise UNKNOWN. Guru Lhakhang at Jampa: Guru Rinpoche flanked by Avalokiteshvara and Amitayus per a snippet only.

**Identification guide (for the animator)**
- Shakyamuni: gold, right hand touches ground, left in lap holding a black begging bowl (HAR item 501). https://www.himalayanart.org/items/501
- Maitreya as Buddha: teaching gesture (HAR set 1754). As bodhisattva: jewels and silks, often relaxed seat, small stupa on crown, legs pendant "Western posture" (HAR outline). https://www.himalayanart.org/news/post.cfm?postID=13 . In HAR 501 he holds two lotus stems supporting a wheel and water flask.
- Dipamkara: teaching gesture, eight-spoked wheels on palms and feet (HAR item 7943), no stupa or flask; HAR admits "process of elimination". https://www.himalayanart.org/items/7943
- Guru Rinpoche: lotus hat topped with a feather, golden vajra at heart, skullcup with long-life vase, khatvanga staff on left shoulder, moustache and small goatee (HAR 538). https://www.himalayanart.org/items/538
- Zhabdrung: "long tapering beard and broad red fan-like meditation hat" (Rubin). http://rubinmuseum.org/objects/1634/zhabdrung-ngawang-namgyal-15941651-with-a-brief-lineage . A search snippet on a SOAS paper says depictions vary and beards alone are not conclusive; not opened.

## 6. Variants and disagreements
- Past Buddha: Dipamkara (Rubin, HAR) vs Kashyapa (Phurdrup; Wikipedia). HAR item 501 equates them in one label; HAR 89120 cannot decide.
- Gestures: HAR's Dipamkara and Maitreya share the teaching gesture. Commercial sellers give blessing hands for Dipamkara (snippet comment only, not opened). Treat HAR as the standard and note identification often depends on inscriptions.
- Buddha Dordenma height: Wikipedia lead 54 m (177 ft) [11]; BBS "201-feet tall" [12]; bhutan.travel 51.5 m by search snippet only. Which Buddha: Wikipedia says Shakyamuni; BBS says only "Buddha Dordenma". Date: Wikipedia lead gives completion 25 September 2015, citing Kuensel; BBS undated "yesterday... after 10 years". Contents: "over 100,000" (BBS) vs 100,000 eight-inch plus 25,000 twelve-inch (Wikipedia lead). Use "more than 100,000 small Buddhas" and avoid a height, or say "about 51 to 54 m depending on source" after reading bhutan.travel.
- Zhabdrung's hat and beard: depictions vary (snippet of a SOAS paper). Birthplace conflicts also exist in some sources; not needed.
- Maitreya's gesture: not obtained from any opened page beyond HAR's Buddha-form teaching gesture. The lead's "bodhisattva vs Buddha" difference is confirmed in HAR outline, but the dharmachakra link for bodhisattva form is UNKNOWN.

## 8. Sensitivities
- Do not call them "gods". "Deity": HAR classifies Buddha figures under "Classification: Deity" in its catalogue, but whether Bhutanese Buddhists accept it is UNKNOWN. Safer: "Buddha", "bodhisattva", "sacred image".
- Do not call them "types". Say "the Buddha of the past, of our age, of the future".
- Etiquette from a tour operator (not an institution): "Avoid touching statues, paintings, ritual objects, altars"; "avoid stretching your legs towards an altar"; clockwise around chortens; "Visitors should not photograph inside sacred sites". https://www.drukasia.com/bhutan-tours/tips/dos-and-donts-in-bhutan-journey . Show feet pointed away from images, sit cross-legged. No rule on placing images "low" found: UNKNOWN; as an editorial choice, keep figures high in frame.
- Disclosure: animation depicts sacred images; check with a Bhutanese consultant on depicting Zhabdrung and Guru Rinpoche faces.

## 9. Memorable details (sourced)
1. Sumedha spreads his hair and bark clothing in the mud and lies face down so Dipamkara can walk across [6].
2. Dipamkara tells him he will be Gotama Buddha "unnumbered cycles hence" [6].
3. Maitreya waits in Tushita, the "fifth of the thousand buddhas of this fortunate eon" [3].
4. Phurdrup's golden Buddhas are made of painted clay, about 122 cm tall, in a Hephu-tradition style [4].
5. Jampa Lhakhang's Maitreya is "probably the earliest grand Buddhist clay sculpture made in Bhutan", yet nobody knows who made it [10].
6. At Punakha Kuenrey a 35-foot Shakyamuni sits between 28-foot Guru Rinpoche and Zhabdrung (raonline).
7. Guru Rinpoche's famous prayer calls him "Embodiment of buddhas of past, present and future" [13].
8. Dipamkara's hands and feet carry eight-spoked wheels; Shakyamuni holds a black bowl, a gift "from the Kings of the Four Directions" (HAR 7943, 501).
