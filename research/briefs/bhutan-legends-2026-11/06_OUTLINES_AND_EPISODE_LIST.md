# 06 Corrected story outlines and proposed episode list (for Sparsh's nod)

Date: 2026-11-11. Status: PROPOSAL. No screenplay work starts until Sparsh approves.
Evidence is in dossiers `01` to `05` in this folder; this file only summarises them.
Tags: [T] = tradition (told as "Bhutanese tradition says"), [H] = documented history.

## How the research was checked

Five Sonnet 5.5 agents wrote the dossiers. The lead then downloaded the main scholarly
sources and confirmed the key quotes itself:

- Ardussi, "Gyalse Tenzin Rabgye and the Founding of Taktsang Lhakhang", JBS 1 (1999): 1692
  foundation, 1694 completion, the local deity's offer to the Zhabdrung. Confirmed.
- Sonam Kinga, "Who is the Tigress in the Lair?", JBS 40 (2019): tigress variants. Confirmed.
- Ardussi, "Formation of the State of Bhutan", JBS 11 (2004): Ralung hierarch, 1616, the
  relic dispute, 1625/26 government, death kept secret "more than fifty years". Confirmed.
- Sonam Kinga, "Dumping the Jewel in the River", JBS 49 (2023): 21st vertebra, the raven,
  invasion years, casualties, the orange, Mo Chhu. Confirmed.
- Himalayan Art Resources set 1754 (gestures of the three Buddhas) and the Punakha Kuenrey
  description. Confirmed.

**Not obtained (honest gaps):** Karma Phuntsho's *History of Bhutan*, Michael Aris, Treasury
of Lives, Britannica, Kuensel's own site and most gov.bt pages refused automated access.
Nothing below rests on them. All pronunciations are unsourced and need a native speaker.

## Where Sparsh's versions were right, wrong or contested

| # | Sparsh's version | Verdict | What the sources say |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1a | "Padmasambhava" is the Hindi name | WRONG | Sanskrit, "Lotus-Born". In Bhutan he is Guru Rinpoche, "Precious Guru". (01) |
| 1b | First Buddhist monk to migrate to Bhutan | WRONG as worded | A tantric master, not a monk; he visited by invitation (Bumthang first, by tradition) and left. He is credited with bringing Buddhism in the 8th century. Year of first visit is not settled in what we could read. (01) |
| 1c | One of three figures in most temples, with the Buddha of the Present and the Founder | PARTLY RIGHT | Shakyamuni with Guru Rinpoche and Zhabdrung is a real, common arrangement (e.g. Punakha Dzong's assembly hall). But other temples differ, and no source says "most". They are not "deities" in the usual sense. (02) |
| 1d | Flew to Tiger's Nest in "Rudra form" | WRONG | The form is Dorje Drolo, one of his eight manifestations. No source links Taktsang to Rudra; probably a mishearing (possibly of "Drolo" or "Drakpo"). (01) |
| 1e | Subdued a demon | PARTLY RIGHT | Tradition speaks of many local gods and spirits, bound to protect the teachings, not one demon destroyed. (01) |
| 1f | Meditated in a cave there | RIGHT [T] | The "3 years, 3 months, 3 weeks, 3 days, 3 hours" figure is only in popular sources. (01) |
| 1g | Founded the monastery | WRONG [H] | The temple was founded in 1692 by Tenzin Rabgye, the 4th Druk Desi, finished 1694; rebuilt after the 1998 fire. (01) |
| 1h | (not in his version) Who the tigress was | CONTESTED | Popular telling: his consort Yeshe Tsogyal. Bhutanese scholar Sonam Kinga: a Bhutanese woman, Tashi Khyidren. One museum caption says Mandarava. (01) |
| 2a | Three "types" of Buddha: past, present, future | PARTLY RIGHT | Three individual Buddhas of successive ages, not types: Dipamkara (sometimes Kashyapa), Shakyamuni, Maitreya. Shared across Buddhism, not special to Bhutan. (02) |
| 2b | Each has its own posture and gesture | PARTLY RIGHT | Shakyamuni touches the earth; Dipamkara and Maitreya usually share the same teaching gesture and are told apart by attributes or inscriptions. (02) |
| 3a | The Founder was a prince and heir of Tibet | WRONG | Zhabdrung Ngawang Namgyal was the hereditary head (hierarch) of Ralung monastery and the Drukpa school, recognised as the rebirth of Pema Karpo. Not heir to any state. (03) |
| 3b | His uncle tried to assassinate him | NO SUPPORT FOUND | No uncle, no assassination attempt in any source read. The threat was the ruler of Tsang, who backed a rival claimant and demanded the relic. We cannot rule out that some oral version has an uncle. (03) |
| 3c | He fled Tibet | RIGHT [H] | In 1616, aged about 22, carrying the relic. (03) |
| 3d | First night, dreamed of a Thunder Dragon flying south | WRONG | Tradition: the protector Mahakala (Yeshe Gonpo) offered him the land in a vision, then flew ahead of him as a raven. No dragon, no "first night". (03) |
| 3e | Arrived in the 15th to 16th century | WRONG | 1616: 17th century. (03) |
| 3f | United warring tribes into one country | PARTLY RIGHT | Not tribes: independent valley communities and rival religious schools, with Drukpa monasteries already there since the 13th century. He did unify them, over about 25 years; the east came last. (03) |
| 3g | "Land of the Thunder Dragon" comes from the dream | WRONG | "Druk" is the Drukpa school, named four centuries earlier after thunder heard as a dragon's roar at its founder's monastery. The founder-story detail rests on weaker sources. (03) |
| 4a | Relic from the 21st backbone of a great guru | RIGHT [T] | Sparsh had this right and the lead's doubt was wrong. Rangjung Kharsapani: 21 images of Avalokiteshvara on 21 vertebrae of Tsangpa Gyare (d. 1211); this one from the 21st. Single scholarly source. (04) |
| 4b | Tibetan armies came for it | RIGHT [H] | Five invasions in the Bhutanese school textbook: 1617, 1634, 1639, 1644, 1648. Later ones were also about politics, not only the relic. (03, 04) |
| 4c | Won without killing anyone | WRONG | A general killed in the first invasion; "thousands of Tibetans killed" at Paro in 1648. Only the Punakha standoff, in oral tradition, was won by a trick. (04) |
| 4d | Threw a replica into the Pho Chhu or Mo Chhu | PARTLY RIGHT | Mo Chhu. Oral tradition: he pretended to throw the relic and threw an orange, with the relic in his sleeve. No written record of it exists; it is folk memory. Year disputed: 1639 or 1649. (04) |
| 4e | The real relic is in Punakha Dzong | RIGHT | (04) |
| 4f | A chamber only the King, the "Dharmaraja" and the caretaker may enter | CONFLATED | That rule is documented for the Machen Lhakhang, which holds the Zhabdrung's body, not the relic: Kings, the Je Khenpo (chief abbot) and the caretaker. Who may enter the relic's room is unknown. "Dharma Raja" is an old British term, not the right word for the Je Khenpo; say "Je Khenpo". (04) |

## Proposed episode list (Bhutan)

Four episodes. Each about 5 minutes except the last.

| Ep | Working title | Covers | Why here |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | The Tigress and the Cliff | Guru Rinpoche, Dorje Drolo, Taktsang; the temple built 900 years later | Most recognisable image of Bhutan; best-sourced; self-contained pilot |
| 2 | The Raven Flies South | Zhabdrung leaves Ralung in 1616 with the relic; the cave; the founding | Part 1 of his life |
| 3 | The Jewel in the River | The invasions, Punakha, the orange, the festival, the 54-year silence | Part 2 of his life; the strongest plot |
| 4 | Who Is on the Altar? (2 to 3 minutes) | The three figures a visitor sees, and the Buddhas of the three times | Explainer; ties 1 to 3 together; also works as Shorts |

Recommendation: make episode 1 first. Alternative: lead with episode 3, which has the best
story, but it needs episode 2 as set-up.

## Outline: Episode 1, The Tigress and the Cliff

1. Cold open: a temple on a cliff face high above the Paro valley. "The story says no one
   climbed here first. He flew."
2. [T] Who he is: Guru Rinpoche, the Lotus-Born, an 8th-century tantric master who brought
   Buddhism to these valleys. Bumthang first: the sick king, the local deity Shelging Karpo
   who becomes a protector.
3. [T] The flight: he takes the wrathful form Dorje Drolo and rides a tigress to the cliff.
   Who was the tigress? Most say his consort Yeshe Tsogyal; a Bhutanese scholar argues she
   was a Bhutanese woman, Tashi Khyidren. We say both.
4. [T] On the cliff: the gods and spirits of the place are bound by oath to protect the
   teachings. Wrath here is compassion, not a monster fight. He meditates in the cave and
   hides teachings for later ages.
5. [H] Nine centuries pass. In the 1640s the Zhabdrung comes to the cave in wartime; the
   deity of the cliff appears to him and offers the place. He never builds the temple.
6. [H] 1692: Tenzin Rabgye, who had been a boy in the Zhabdrung's following, stands at the
   cave mouth and orders the foundation laid. Finished 1694. Tradition records a rain of
   flowers and no one hurt on the cliff paths.
7. [H] 1998: fire. Rebuilt by 2005. Close: the building is three centuries old, has burned
   and returned; the story is twelve centuries old and has not changed.

Avoid: "founded the monastery", "demon", "Rudra", a fixed first-visit year, the 3-3-3-3-3
figure unless framed as popular tradition.

## Outline: Episode 2, The Raven Flies South

1. Cold open: 1616, a 22-year-old abbot leaves his monastery carrying a wrapped bundle.
2. [H] Ralung, Tibet. Born 1594 into the family that has led the Drukpa school for four
   centuries. Recognised as the rebirth of the great scholar Pema Karpo. A rival claimant,
   backed by the ruler of Tsang.
3. [T/H] The bundle: the Rangjung Kharsapani, an image of the Buddha of Compassion said
   to have formed on the 21st vertebra of the school's founder. It is used to confirm each
   rebirth. The ruler orders him to hand it over. He refuses.
4. [T] The vision: the protector Mahakala offers him the southern valleys, then goes ahead
   of him as a raven.
5. [H] The south: valleys with no single ruler, rival schools, and Drukpa patrons who take
   him in. The first army from Tsang is beaten back.
6. [H] The cave north of Thimphu, three years: stay a wandering hermit, or build a state?
   He chooses. Late 1620s: the "Sixteen I" declaration. 1627: two Portuguese Jesuits spend
   months with him and describe him. 1629: Simtokha, the first dzong.
7. Where the name comes from: "Druk", the thunder dragon, is the name of his school, older
   than him by four centuries. He gave the dragon a country. Cliffhanger: the armies return.

Avoid: "prince", "heir of Tibet", any uncle, a dragon dream, "tribes", Bhutan-versus-Tibet
framing (it is a succession dispute inside one religious school, then a regional war).

## Outline: Episode 3, The Jewel in the River

1. Cold open: every year at Punakha, a procession walks to the river and oranges are thrown
   in. Young men dive for them.
2. [H] The wars: 1634, Simtokha is taken and lost when its powder store explodes. 1639.
   1644: an army disarmed and sent home. People died in these wars; we say so.
3. [H] Punakha Dzong, built at the meeting of the Pho Chhu and Mo Chhu (begun late 1630s).
   1640: the dual system, a chief abbot and a civil ruler.
4. [H/T] Winter 1648: two armies. At Punakha the defenders march out of one gate and back
   in by another, an army without end.
5. [T] The river: the Zhabdrung announces that the cause of the war will be thrown away.
   A procession to the Mo Chhu. Something falls into the water. The besiegers go home. It
   was an orange; the relic was in his sleeve. (Told as folk memory: no written record;
   some date it to 1639.)
6. [H] 1649: the first thanksgiving festival, still held, with the village militia, the
   pazaps, in the dress of that war.
7. [H] 1651: he enters retreat in Punakha Dzong and dies. Those closest to him keep it
   quiet for more than fifty years, so the young state holds together. His body rests there
   still, in a room only the Kings, the Je Khenpo and its caretaker enter. The relic is in
   the same dzong. Neither is shown on screen.

Avoid: "no one was killed", "replica", Pho Chhu as the river, "Dharmaraja", "cover-up",
any close-up of the relic, the interior of the Machen, later Zhabdrung incarnations.

## Outline: Episode 4, Who Is on the Altar?

1. Walk into a Bhutanese temple hall: three large figures. Who are they?
2. Often: Shakyamuni, the Buddha of our age, in the centre, touching the earth; Guru
   Rinpoche, with the lotus hat and staff; the Zhabdrung, with the long beard and red hat.
   The teaching, the one who brought it, the one who built a country around it.
3. Not always: some temples centre Guru Rinpoche; arrangements vary.
4. A different three: the Buddhas of the three times. Dipamkara before, Shakyamuni now,
   Maitreya to come. One short legend: Sumedha lying in the mud so Dipamkara can cross.
5. How to tell them apart, honestly: Shakyamuni is easy; the other two share a gesture.
6. Close: Guru Rinpoche himself is called "the Buddha of the three times" in a well-known
   prayer, which is why the ideas blur.
