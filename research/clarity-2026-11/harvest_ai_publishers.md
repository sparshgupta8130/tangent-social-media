# Harvest: AI news / explainer pages and publishers (carousel-heavy)

Collected 2026-11-15 via Claude in Chrome (read-only). Slide content was read from screenshots of every slide I list (the image URLs are blocked from tool output, so no curl downloads; screenshots only). Where I list "slides seen", that is what I looked at. Word counts are approximate. Dates are relative ("1d") as shown by IG; the environment date is 2026-11-15.

Method note: the post page shows likes/comments in the action bar. Engagement figures are as displayed on that day.

## 1. @therundownai (666K followers; "Media/news company", 3,080 posts)
Why picked: biggest AI-news explainer brand; text-first news posts, so a good baseline for "news with a headline on the image".
Format mix on the grid (first 36): ~2/3 single image, ~1/3 carousel. Carousels are mostly 2-6 slides of tweet screenshots or video stills.

### R1. https://www.instagram.com/p/DfsfvhCSHFM/  (1d, single image, 221 likes / 29 comments)

- Audience: AI-curious general reader.
- Readability: grade ~10; no jargon; fully literal; one long sentence but a plain subject-verb-object.
- Hook: literal news statement, no cleverness. Stops the thumb via recognizable brand names + a conflict ("sued").
- Caption: ~900 chars. Starts "A coalition of consumer advocacy and online safety groups has filed a lawsuit against OpenAI, Google, Meta, Anthropic, and SpaceXSI, seeking to stop them from developing or releasing new AI models." Restates then extends (who, where, why, legal basis), plain short paragraphs, CTA "Follow us (@therundownAI)", "Source: Associated Press". No hashtags. No AI disclosure.
- Clarity: 9. The headline IS the whole story.

### Account patterns (@therundownai)
- [OBSERVED] Template is fixed: small category tag ("AI NEWS") + 1 headline of 16-25 words in bottom-left over a darkened photo, logo top-left. Same look every time = instant recognition.
- [OBSERVED] Headlines are complete literal sentences with named actors and a verb ("X are being sued by Y"). No metaphor.

- [INFERENCE] Engagement is mostly 200-650 likes on a 666K account, so this brand is not winning on per-post virality; it wins on consistency and a clear headline.

7. **Hooks.** [OBSERVED] Types: concrete event + name (R1, A1, E2); "this person built X to help Y" (O1); question with concrete fragments (W1 "NO PILOTS. FEW AIR TRAFFIC CONTROLLERS. # CROSS-ACCOUNT LESSONS (UPDATED, WHOLE SET; supersedes the earlier lessons list where they differ)

Evidence tags: [OBSERVED] seen on the post; [INFERENCE] my reasoning.

## 1. Engagement is mostly not a clarity signal; use it with caution
[OBSERVED] The highest-liked posts in this set are a CEO-fan photo dump with no text (N2 59.6K), a launch carousel on a famous brand (D4 51.5K, D2 20.4K), a celebrity-reaction list (E2 24.7K) and a scandal explainer (E1 11K). [INFERENCE] Brand, celebrity and news timing drive reach; clarity decides whether viewers who landed on the post understand it. So the clarity lessons below come from comparing posts INSIDE the same account (same audience), listed in each pair above, and from reading each slide.

## 2. Words per slide (all counts from slides I read)
- Hook slide: 6-19 words. Cleanest: 6-12 (E2 6, N1 17 split into an 11-word lead + 6-word headline, O3 12, O6 14, O1 12, D2 7, X1 10). The 17-19-word headlines (T1, T3) were less clear when the topic depended on prior news (T3).
- Body slides: 12-35 words is the sweet spot among the clearest posts: O1 9-17, D4 8-14 (!), D3 15-20, D1 20-35, O6 20-30, O3 25-50 as bullets, E1 25-45, A1 ~20. Slides over 50 words appear at Wired (W1 up to 60), Axios (X3 ~55), N1 S3 (55) and O5 tweets (60-70): these are the "read the article" slides and were the least clear.
- [OBSERVED] The single-shortest posts (D4 with 8-14 words a slide) got the highest like count among body-text carousels (51.5K) - confounded by brand.

## 3. Number of slides and the end of the arc
- [OBSERVED] 2-3 slides for news + proof (A1, A2, R2, X1-X2 single, V1, N1, T2, T3); 4-5 for stories (O1, O3, O4, O6, D2, D5, N2, X3); 6-9 for lists/stories (R3, E1, W1, D1, D3, D4 7, T1 8, O5 7); 20 for a list of reactions (E2).
- [OBSERVED] How arcs END (slides 6-9): 
  (a) a separate CTA poster slide: Evolving E1 S9 ("Join the #1 AI Newsletter... Subscribe for Free, LINK IN BIO"), Rundown R3 S6 (newsletter poster), TheAIField T1 S8 and T3 S3 (newsletter poster).
  (b) a text CTA over the hook image: DeepMind D1 S9 "Tap the link in bio to explore Gemini Robotics 2.5"; D2/D3/D5 "More at the link in bio" with an availability sentence; Axios X3 S5 "Tap the link in bio for more."
  (c) no CTA, the arc ends on the content: E2 (a pro-open-source tweet), W1 (a closing quote), W2 (closing quote), O3 (comfort line), O6 (4th call word), O4 (finished bed photo), O2 (collage).
  (d) a payoff/status slide before the CTA: E1 S8 "Now Jensen is out, and Colette Kress... is running Nvidia"; D1 S6 "...96% success rate", S8 hazard line.
  [INFERENCE] OpenAI never uses a CTA slide and puts the whole call-to-action in the caption/brand; news/media pages always add the newsletter CTA on the last slide. ## 4. Where the headline sits against the visual
[OBSERVED] Bottom third, left-aligned or centred, over a darkened image: E1, E2, A1, A2, T1, T3, R1, O1, O3, O4, O6, X1, X3, N1. ## 5. Headlines: literal event statements, or a person plus benefit
[OBSERVED] The clearest headlines state who did what or the benefit: "Trump replaces chief of staff Susie Wiles with Stephen Miller" (X1), "This legend built an app to help your grandparents not get scammed" (O1), "This computer scientist is learning how to speak 'elephant' to warn them of danger" (O6), "IVF is complex. Weak ones: the metaphor "THE END OF THE FOUNDER FAIRY TALE" (W2); the bare product-version title with no benefit ("Gemini 3.8 Live and 3.8 Live Extended Thinking", D5); headlines needing the previous news ("AND THIS ONE HAS NOTHING TO DO WITH JENSEN'S AFFAIRS", E1; T3).
[INFERENCE] A cold viewer should be able to retell the headline to a friend. "A person + a thing they made/did + why it matters" passes; "Product X version" and metaphors fail.

## 6. Jargon: five strategies seen
1. Avoid it (O1, O6, X1).
2. Restate in an everyday verb inside the same sentence (D1 "messing up a task, figures out what went wrong", "virtual practice space", O3 "progesterone-dominant state... it wants calm, warmth, and steadiness").
3. Define in brackets/inline (O6 "caretaker (mahout)", E1 "guarantees for AI companies that couldn't afford its chips upfront").
4. Give the term its own slide as the headline (W1 "eVTOLs / electric vertical takeoff and landing aircraft"; O6 uses "Trumpet / Roar / Rumble" as card titles - the glossary format).
5. Let a screenshot carry it (R3, E1, T1) - least reliable; failed with "=LET(...)" (R3), "frontier model/agentic" (D2), "DCCC" (X3).
Technical specs and benchmarks stay in the caption or on "proof" slides that nobody has to read (D2 S3, D5 S3).

## 7. Cohesion: how slides chain
[OBSERVED] No page used "but / so / here's why" bridges. The glue was: (a) a fixed frame on every slide; (b) a repeated subject as the first word ("Matt ...", O1); (c) chronology/steps (O1, O5 "1/6...6/6", T1 tweets in order, O4 Step 1-3 in caption); (d) numbered list ("1. The Texas trifecta", X3); (e) bold-lead lines that read as a summary on their own (D1, D3, D4); (f) a recurring kicker ("CLIPPED WINGS", W1); (g) glossary cards (O6). Progress dots always there.

## 8. Captions
[OBSERVED] News pages restate the hook in sentence 1 then add 3-5 short paragraphs: E3, R1, R2, A2, X1 (Axios adds "WHY IT MATTERS"). Publishers (Wired, Verge) write a separate narrative lede. OpenAI writes the human story in prose (1,000-1,500 chars; O5 is "217 years later..." only). DeepMind: 230-500 chars, product + what it does, "Learn more at the link in bio", sometimes a 4-item emoji bullet list (D5). Nvidia: opens on a human sentence (N1, N2). CTAs: newsletter/follow (Rundown, TAF, Evolving), link in bio (Wired, Verge, Axios, DeepMind), opinion question (A1, A2), none (OpenAI). No hashtags on any post; AI disclosure only incidental ("these are AI renders", O2).

## Template 3: "Chat as story" (5 slides) - source E3 https://www.instagram.com/p/DfwzCiPgM8u/ and O3 https://www.instagram.com/p/Dfcc4VhG04j/
1. Hook: "<Result>, but <twist>" 14 words in caps or sentence case, one image.
2-4. Slides = real turns of the AI chat as bubbles (15-45 words), each a step; a hand-drawn underline or arrow highlights the key line (O3 red arrow, E3 red underline).
5. The punchline/closing sentence or a calm answer ("Your body is..."), with the term bolded and explained by the rest of the sentence.
Use for: showing the real prompt and what the AI answered about a photo edit or an astrology question.

## Template 4: "Problem -> why it's hard -> idea -> proof -> where" (6-9 slides) - source D1 https://www.instagram.com/p/DfkL1aEERT1/
1. Product + one plain promise with two concrete examples.
2. Tension in human terms: "X feels effortless to us. But for <the AI> it's one of the hardest..."
3. The one idea ("one brain for hundreds of tasks").
4-5. How it works in everyday verbs ("watches replays... figures out what went wrong").
6. A real result with a number ("96% success rate").
7. A safety/limitation line (trust).
8. Closing line + CTA over the hook image.
Bold lead line on each slide so the bold lines alone tell the story.

## Template 5: "Headline + number card + one-line verdict" (2-3 slides) - source A1 https://www.instagram.com/p/DfwcFnQlRKq/ and V1 https://www.instagram.com/p/Dfr9UlvlZvz/
1. Verdict headline (10-14 words), the key phrase/number highlighted in an accent colour; swipe pill.
2. Scorecard: 4-6 big numbers with 2-word labels (A1), or a Good/Bad two-column box with a score badge (V1).
3. One-sentence takeaway over a photo, or "Read the full thing at the link in bio".
Use for: a before/after photo-edit comparison or "I tested N ways" results (numbers instead of metaphors).

## Anti-templates (do not copy)
- Metaphor headline (W2); bare version-number title (D5); decorative benchmark tables (D2 S3); 50-60-word paragraph slides (W1 S7-S8, O5); headlines that depend on prior news (E1 S1, T3); photo dumps with no text where the story lives only in the caption (N2, O4 slides 2-4) unless the audience already loves the person.
