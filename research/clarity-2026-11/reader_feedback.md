## 2. Her points, mapped to slides

### Point 1: Overall content is "very complex" (clip B)
- Concerns: the deleted carousel throughout (slide 2 equinox vs fixed stars and 24 degrees; slide 3 "deterministic code", "source badges"; slide 4 arcsecond, NASA JPL, Swiss Ephemeris, almanac; slide 5 red-team, pre-filter, streaming guard). Partly the launch carousel's slide 9.
- Wanted: simpler content she can follow without prior knowledge.
- Confused or bored by: density and specialist vocabulary; numbers with no plain meaning (27 of 28, 6 s, 1 arcsecond, 427, 113).
- Liked: nothing explicitly praised.
- Principle: clarity and jargon; the reader's knowledge, not the writer's, sets the level.

### Point 2: The cover must say exactly what the post is about, in very few simple words (clip B)
- Concerns: deleted slide 1 ("The AI kept moving Saturn. So I took the maths away from it." with "Western: Aries / Vedic: Pisces" diagram) and launch slide 1 (evocative, not descriptive).
- Wanted: to know what to expect before deciding to swipe.
- Confused by: a cover that is a hook or a mood, not a label. Saturn, maths, and "it" are all unexplained to a stranger.
- Principle: missing "what is this and why should I care"; the cover is a promise/contract, not a teaser.

### Point 3: Every slide needs one core message or one-liner saying what the slide is about (clip B)
- Concerns: deleted slides 2 to 5 (headlines are stylish phrases such as "One sky, two ways to count it" and "reference books"; the actual point sits in small body text). Launch slides 3 to 8 are the model of what she wants.
- Wanted: a headline that is itself the message; example given: one feature per slide for Panditji, then detail underneath.
- Principle: visual hierarchy and slide-level clarity; headline = takeaway, body = optional depth.

### Point 4: Less content-heavy, but heavy is fine if there is one core message (clip B)
- Wanted: permission to keep depth, as long as it hangs off one line. Not a demand to cut everything.
- Principle: layered reading (headline for skimmers, body for the interested). Too much text matters only when no single line leads.

### Point 5: Interested readers read on; uninterested can scroll past and still get the message (clip B)
- Wanted: each slide to be useful to a skimmer. If she skips the body she should still leave with the gist.
- Principle: skim-ability and flow between slides; each slide must stand alone. This is a design requirement, not a nicety.

### Point 6: Colors: white on black, more white, less black (clip B; flagged as her guess, "research about it")
- Concerns: both carousels use dark backgrounds with gold/pink/green text and dim grey small text; the deleted slides in particular have low-contrast body text and a lot of dark space.
- Wanted: stronger contrast, more white (could mean white text, white areas, or both: ambiguous).
- Principle: legibility and contrast. Her wording is tentative; treat it as a hypothesis to test, not a rule.

### Point 7: Tone is "very presentation-ish" (clip A)
- Concerns: the voice of the headlines and one-liners overall.
- Wanted: it should sound like the Tangent owner or an influencer talking to an audience, either witty or conversational/direct; experiment with tones, maybe one witty and one direct.
- Confused or bored by: lines that read like a talk-slide or conference deck.
- Principle: tone and voice; human, person-to-person, not polished corporate. Note: this partly conflicts with the "stylish headline" diagnosis, since polish is the problem, not a cure.

## 3. Beyond "the headlines were too stylish" (most valuable)

1. The cover is a different job from the other slides. It must label the post, not hook with mood.
2. Skimmer-safe design: a slide should deliver its message to someone who reads only the headline. Her test is "can I scroll past and still know what you were trying to say?"
3. Depth is allowed. She does not ask for fewer words overall; she asks for one anchor line per slide. This pushes back on "keep it minimal".
4. Tone is a separate axis from clarity: even plain headlines can feel like a presentation. She wants a person talking, and suggests running different tones as experiments.
5. Colour: more white, less black. Unverified; worth a quick A/B.
6. Expect-before-swipe: she frames the cover as setting expectations so she can decide whether to invest. Trust and interest both hang on that.
7. She gave no feedback on the technical proof content (numbers, benchmarks) itself, only on how it reads. Nothing in the audio says proof-style slides are unwanted; they just need a plain headline.

## 4. Against or with common creator advice

- Reinforces: clear cover promise; one idea per slide; skim-ability; person-to-person voice.
- Conflicts with: the "curiosity gap" cover (tease, do not tell) and with polished editorial serif headlines as a default; both are exactly what she found opaque. Also sits against "text-light at all costs": she tolerates dense slides if anchored.
- Neutral or unverified: white-on-black. Dark mode is currently fashionable for tech accounts; she suggests more white, so treat as a test, not a ruling.

## 5. Rules this feedback implies

1. Cover = plain label of the post in a few simple words (what it is and who it is for). No mood, no riddle.
2. Every slide headline states the slide's single point in plain words a non-expert would use. If the headline were the only thing read, the message must survive.
3. One idea per slide; supporting detail below it is optional depth, never the point.
4. No jargon in headlines (maths, deterministic, arcsecond, ephemeris, red-team, model). Explain or drop; put specifics in the body if at all.
5. Numbers need a plain-language meaning next to them.
6. Voice: a person speaking to an audience, conversational or witty or direct; avoid presentation phrasing and slogan-like one-liners. Test more than one tone.
7. Maximise contrast: bigger/whiter text, less dim grey and less empty dark; test white-heavy variants.
8. Check each draft by showing a non-tech first-time reader the cover only and asking what they expect; then each headline only.
9. Treat her as the primary acceptance test before posting.
