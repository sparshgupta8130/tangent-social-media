# Harvest: AI/tech Shorts by creators who make it simple (YouTube Shorts slice)

Collected 2026-11-15 via Claude in Chrome, read-only. Source for every video is the YouTube watch page and its transcript panel.

## Scope and limits (read first)
- 7 creators, 17 videos with full transcripts. Target was about 25, so this is short of it. Output size limits of the browser tool made each video cost several calls.
- Instagram was NOT harvested by me. Other agents were using Instagram tabs; I stayed on YouTube. No on-screen cover/poster downloads were made. On-screen text was observed on frames for 4 videos only (Cleo supersonic, Varun Jensen, Ishan Claude skill, Kallaway researcher). Elsewhere on-screen text is marked "not observed".
- Words per minute (wpm), sentence length and grade level are my rough hand counts from the transcripts, not tool output. Treat as plus or minus 10 percent. Likes, comment counts: comments not captured. Auto-dubbed audio and auto-captions mean transcript typos are YouTube's.
- No captcha, login wall, or warning was hit. Nothing was liked, followed, or commented.
- Candidates dropped: @RealRileyBrown on YouTube is a different channel (gaming and ad clips); the AI builder is @rileybrownai. Kallaway's top Shorts are Microsoft Copilot Studio sponsored videos (#CopilotPartner), so they are paid work. Noted where relevant.
- MKBHD's recent Shorts are gadget content, not AI. Included for craft only (hook and pacing). @cleoabram's Shorts are science. Included for craft; she is the clearest benchmark for plain language.
- Indian audience: Varun Mayya and Ishan Sharma (both India-based, Hinglish-adjacent but these Shorts are in plain English). Harper Carroll and Tiff In Tech are US-facing.

Subscriber counts at time of visit: Cleo Abram 8.96M; MKBHD 21.4M; Varun Mayya 1.19M; Ishan Sharma 2.19M; Tiff In Tech 654K; Riley Brown 314K; Harper Carroll AI 65.1K; Kallaway 40.4K (his Instagram card in the video shows 644K followers).

# Creator patterns (one per creator)

1. **Cleo Abram**: Concrete claim or a game ("guess") in line one, short sentences about a picture the viewer can see, then a question that works as the CTA ("To see that, subscribe"). Highest clarity in the set.
2. **MKBHD**: A 3-5 word title that names the thing. First spoken line says what the thing does in plain words and calls it "mind-blowing". Then price, specs, and a hands-on moment. Relaxed pace.
3. **Varun Mayya**: Breaking-news first line, name plus one red word on screen, chain of dated facts, no CTA. Best Shorts ride a big, timely event.

# Cross-creator lessons

1. **The strongest hooks name a concrete thing or a surprising claim, not an idea.** Examples: "This camera allows you to see sound in real time" (https://www.youtube.com/shorts/ai1gIWacf0I, 2.7M), "This plane can go faster than sound with no audible sonic boom" (https://www.youtube.com/shorts/sY4SaeDwM_Y), "I just built this with Opus 5.5... Bluey" (https://www.youtube.com/shorts/IEYzYopNGVQ, 630k), "Breaking news. Jensen Huang just resigned" (https://www.youtube.com/shorts/dmSAVk8vwTw, 939k). Weakest hook: a name-chain with a metaphor title (https://www.youtube.com/shorts/JzNqZxkq1_o, 3.8k). [OBSERVED]
2. **Plain script: 5th-8th grade, 12-17 words per sentence at 140-200 wpm.** Cleo is the clearest at grade 5-6 and 175-205 wpm. Fast pace works only when sentences stay short. The one 225 wpm, 30-word-sentence script (Harper sparring partner) was the worst. [OBSERVED counts, rough]
3. **Define jargon in one short clause or by example, or show it.** "Agents are just digital assistants that do tasks for you" (https://www.youtube.com/shorts/zFcsXAEfKTE); "a monorepo... a desktop app and an iOS app that connect" (https://www.youtube.com/shorts/IEYzYopNGVQ); "10k skill pack" then each skill named and glossed (https://www.youtube.com/shorts/RePzZ_ZNIBM). Where jargon went undefined ("harness... infrastructure") the explanation still needed prior knowledge (https://www.youtube.com/shorts/2rn1Zmrdy7s). [OBSERVED]
4. **Metaphors that work are pictures, not ideas.** Basketball, pin, basketball court for scale (Cleo sun); "an underwaterfall" (Cleo Antarctica); "a city that only lights the room someone's standing in" (Tiff); "Apple fired Steve Jobs" (Varun). Metaphors that fail are abstractions about thinking ("sparring partner", "heartbeat of the world" is borderline-ok because it is one stock clause). The carousel problem reappears in the Harper title. [OBSERVED / INFERENCE]
5. **Beat structure is the same across the best: claim or result first, 2-4 steps or demos, one visible proof, a payoff line.** Cleo sun: 0:00 claim / 0:08 guess / 0:13 steps / 0:23 image / 0:31 payoff (https://www.youtube.com/shorts/DcAjv8YRKN8). Kallaway: result, then step 1/2/3, then recap (https://www.youtube.com/shorts/zFcsXAEfKTE). Varun Fable: numbered list one to five, then "the part that gets me is the range" (https://www.youtube.com/shorts/vzO_Tc9AQ10). First reveal lands within the first 8-15 seconds in every strong video. [OBSERVED]
6. **Bridges are plain and spoken**: "But", "So", "Now,", "Then", "Number two", "Step one", "And my favorite". Kallaway's "here's the thing / here's where it gets fun" appear about every 20-30 seconds as re-hooks. No poetic bridges. [OBSERVED]
7. **On-screen text is short and mostly duplicates speech.** Karaoke captions of 1-5 words (Cleo, Ishan, Varun) in the lower third or center; a single bold headline card (Varun "RESIGNED") or label ("Skill #1", "MACH: 0.97", "LONDON") adds new info. Almost nothing carries cards over 6 words. The video makes sense with sound off only where captions plus labels cover the arc (Cleo, Varun headline). [OBSERVED on 4 videos, INFERENCE about general case]
8. **"I built X with AI" stories for a general audience: show the finished thing first, say it in "I built this" words, then demo by plain commands.** Riley's Bluey (630k) leads with the working app and "no coding knowledge", and narrates each step as a request and result. Kallaway leads with the result "saved me dozens of hours per month", then 3 steps. [OBSERVED]
9. **Endings: the winners use a next-video cliffhanger (Cleo), a feature count (Ishan, "seven more skills"), or just stop on the last fact (Varun).** "Follow for more" is absent from every top video. [OBSERVED]
10. **Titles: 3-8 words, name the thing, no coined words.** Strong: "This can SEE Sound!", "Guess How Far The Sun Is", "Jensen Huang just resigned as CEO of Nvidia". Weak: "Graduate From Claude... To Claude Code" (9.7k), "Have you tried this?" (11k), "Use AI as a Sparring Partner for Your Thinking" (3.8k), "Google Search might be in trouble" (5.8k), and Harper's "Could AI Revolutionize Experiment Selection for Researchers?" (2k). Question form is fine only when it is the question a normal person asks ("Why Is Everyone Buying Mac Minis for AI?" 253k). [OBSERVED views; cause INFERENCE]
11. **Top vs weak, same creator:** Tiff (Zuckerberg quote 596k vs brain 20 watts 5k), Harper (Mac minis 253k vs sparring partner 3.8k), Riley (Bluey 630k vs "Claude to Claude Code" 9.7k), Ishan (Apple unboxing 171k-2.6M vs hiring tool 14k), Kallaway (agents how-to 930k vs "Google Search might be in trouble" 5.8k). The weak ones either open with abstract ideas, stack numbers, assume vocabulary, or have no visible thing. Caveat: reach also depends on topic fame, sponsorship, and the channel's date. [INFERENCE]
12. **Length:** the 0.9-1.1M videos run 38s (Cleo), 77s (Varun), 111s (Kallaway) and 90s (MKBHD), so length alone is not the driver; every sentence still adds a new fact. The 100s Tiff video with 11 numbers lost people. [INFERENCE]

# Rules a scriptwriter could follow
1. Line one says what the thing is or does, in words a 12-year-old knows. No metaphor, no name chain, no setup. (https://www.youtube.com/shorts/ai1gIWacf0I)
2. If you made it, show the finished thing in the first 3 seconds and say "I built this". (https://www.youtube.com/shorts/IEYzYopNGVQ)
3. Titles: 3-8 words, name the thing, no coined words, no riddle. Use a question only if a normal person would ask it.
4. Keep sentences to about 15 words. One fact per sentence. Max 2-3 numbers per 30 seconds.
5. Define each jargon word in 5-10 words the first time, or show it, or cut it.
6. Use one picture-type metaphor at most, only when it makes size or mechanism visible. Never a metaphor about ideas.
7. Beat map: claim, 2-4 steps, one visible proof, one payoff sentence. Reveal the main surprise within 15 seconds.
8. Bridges are plain: "But", "So", "Now", "Then", "Number two", "Step one". No lyrical connectors.
9. On-screen text: 1-5 word captions that match speech, plus one short label for each new item or number. Keep any card under 6 words.
10. Ask the viewer to guess before the reveal when there is a number (Cleo sun).
11. End on a next-video tease or the last fact. Skip "follow for more".
12. Put the plain-language first sentence at the top of the caption so the "more" tap shows the idea; do not lead with links (Ishan's caption leads with social links and loses this).

## Reel skeletons Tangent could borrow
Timestamps are approximate for a 30-45s reel.

**Skeleton A: "Topic pill plus 3-step demo"** (Ishan Apple Mac, https://www.instagram.com/reel/DfC-XtsoeZC/)
- 0-1s: a headline pill ("New Touchscreen Macbook!") that stays on screen the whole time, over the object. Caption 3-6 words.
- 1-8s: "Here's your first look at..." and the main object in plain words.
- 8-25s: 2-3 proofs as visuals (hands on screen, an app open), key words highlighted.
- 25-33s: a local question to the audience ("How much do you think this will cost in India?"). No "follow".

**Skeleton B: "Claim, guess, ladder, image, tease"** (Cleo sun https://www.youtube.com/shorts/DcAjv8YRKN8 and Cleo "You're dead" https://www.instagram.com/reel/DfKroAgIzjF/)
- 0-3s: a concrete claim or a joke plus question ("So you're dead... my condolences."), with the thing on screen.
- 3-10s: ask the viewer to guess or remember.
- 10-25s: 3-4 plain steps, each with a big label card (a day range, a count).
- 25-35s: one picture that makes scale or mechanism visible.
- Last 3s: a cliffhanger question pointing at the next video, not "follow".

**Skeleton C: "Result first, then three named steps"** (Kallaway https://www.youtube.com/shorts/zFcsXAEfKTE; Riley phone https://www.instagram.com/reel/Deb3ZCdgFjW/)
- 0-5s: the finished result ("You can now give Claude a phone, in five minutes").
- 5-15s: why it matters in one sentence.
- 15-60s: "Step one / two / three", each named on screen with one plain line. Screen recording or slide above the face.
- Last 5s: a one-sentence recap. Skip the keyword-DM ask.

**Skeleton D: "Headline plus chain of proofs"** (Varun Jensen, https://www.instagram.com/reel/DfpfGwJhtN6/)
- 0-3s: a headline card on a person (big name plus one red word).
- 3-15s: one analogy anyone knows.
- 15-60s: dated facts, each over a real screenshot or clip, 1-3-word yellow captions.
- End on the last fact. Works for timely events; for Tangent the equivalent is a "new thing I made" moment.

**Skeleton E: "Look at the object" (quiet, premium)** (MKBHD seeing sound, https://www.instagram.com/reel/DfAtZXGRAai/)
- 0-3s: the object on screen plus a 2-word centered caption ("THIS CAMERA").
- 3-15s: what it does, one line at a time, demo on top.
- 15-40s: proof it exists (listing, price) and one honest limitation.
- End: a short human line, no CTA. This leans on existing trust, so a new account should put the whole idea in caption line one.

## Updated rules a scriptwriter could follow (additions)
13. Keep a topic label on screen for the whole reel when the story has 3 or more beats (Skeleton A).
14. Captions: 2-6 words at a time, centered or lower-middle, one highlighted word, never clipped at the edge (Riley's clipped captions failed).
15. Make the caption's first line the plain headline or the question the viewer would ask, under about 20 words.
16. Keep reels under about 70 seconds unless every 10 seconds adds a new fact.
